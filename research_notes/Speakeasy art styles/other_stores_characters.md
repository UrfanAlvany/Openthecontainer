# Characters beyond the Unity Asset Store — 1920s speakeasy

Research date: **18 October 2026**. Target: commercial first-person PC business sim, Unity 6.3 LTS / URP 17; director develops on macOS. Art direction: semi-realistic proportions, photographic/PBR textures, game-level faces. Three parallel researchers covered the store groups; additional research covered garment commissions and a Mac-compatible body workflow. Existing `styles.md` was skimmed; its known Unity Asset Store shortlist was not researched again.

**Recommendation: buy period clothing sources, then commission a small coherent wardrobe on a shared rig.** This search did not verify a new, inexpensive, ready-to-import pack covering flappers, cloche-wearing patrons, period waitresses, cigarette girls and singers together. There are useful single garments and staff/police characters. Generic scans supply convincing people but mostly modern clothing. For a Mac-only workflow, MPFB/Blender is a promising free full-body starting point; for broader existing period wardrobe, a licensed Windows Character Creator contractor can assemble/export Reallusion clothes.

## 2. Best way to get the women

**Mac-only route:** choose one complete rigged female body and commission four coordinated ensembles: flapper, day dress/cloche, waitress/apron, cigarette girl/tray. Use the $29 flapper and $15 cloche as optional source meshes, or commission originals if conversion costs more. MPFB is a free, redistributable base for the artist to work on; a purchased base is also viable if its licence permits contractor access and it has the needed hidden body geometry. Add a singer variation and police uniform for six outfits. Commission bobbed hair and hats as part of the silhouette, not as an afterthought. This is a proposed production approach, not a claim the listed meshes already fit one another.

**Cheaper wardrobe assembly if Windows help is available:** Sckript + BBAlley supply a broad existing 1920s clothing set for roughly **$60–65 in content**, before CC software/contractor costs. Have a licensed artist deliver optimized FBX characters and texture maps that can be imported on the director's Mac. The current Reallusion EULA permits Standard-licensed game use; original source projects and multi-user content access still have restrictions. No contractor quote for this exact assembly was obtained. Direct ActorCore downloads are Mac-friendly, but ActorCore's modern prebuilt actors do not replace this CC wardrobe/export step.

**Budget:** plan **$300–600 per complete fitted, textured and tested outfit**, or **$1,500–4,500 for 4–6 outfits including 25% contingency**. This is an estimate derived from the real listings in section 8, not a fixed vendor offer. A $30–100 entry gig often covers one garment, basic geometry or minor fitting. Start with one complete outfit pilot and evaluate walking, sitting, tray carrying and dancing in Unity before expanding.

**Jazz musicians:** no verified ready-made 1920s band emerged. Reuse the same period suit/waistcoat and singer wardrobe; source instruments and author instrument-specific hand/face/contact animation separately. Jazz dance motions do not provide a jazz band cast.

### Licence evidence

- [CGTrader official Royalty Free help](https://help.cgtrader.com/hc/en-us/articles/360015124437-Royalty-Free-License) expressly permits game integration in proprietary formats and requires reasonable prevention of raw-asset extraction; raw redistribution/resale is prohibited. [General terms](https://www.cgtrader.com/pages/terms-and-conditions) §20.5 permit sharing inside the buying legal entity; no numerical per-seat cap. §19A.12 is a major **subscription trap**: after expiry, downloaded subscription assets remain usable only in projects completed while subscribed; works in progress are excluded. Buy individual licences or keep the subscription active through completion.
- [TurboSquid official licence](https://www.turbosquid.com/licensing) and [official FAQ](https://www.turbosquid.com/help/en/articles/9937423-royalty-free-license-faq): standard/royalty-free models may be embedded in commercial games, protected from raw extraction; the software must not expose model import/export. One company purchase covers employees, parent and majority-owned affiliates; separate clients receiving raw files need their own licences. Editorial-only is unsuitable. Enhanced-price tiers primarily add protections and assignment rights; they are not a separate mandatory game licence.
- [ArtStation stock-asset EUA](https://www.artstation.com/marketplace-product-eula) §4(c) explicitly includes commercial computer games, company employee access and controlled sharing with project collaborators. No numeric seat cap for stock assets; tools/tutorials use different seat rules. Standard allows one commercial work up to 2,000 sales / 20,000 monthly views; Extended removes those caps. [Current official buyer FAQ](https://help.artstation.com/en/articles/16155217-general-faq-for-buyers) says actual EUA controls over contradictory description text. Custom seller EUA must still be checked.

### Licences and technical caveats

**Reallusion licence detail.** Current EULA grants normal commercial games, adaptation and unlimited CC character outputs under Standard. Restrictions cover raw redistribution, paid downloadable content and character generation, plus broadly worded “embedded content within applications or online services”; §2.1(B) expressly permits commercial games. This NPC recommendation does not clear paid costume DLC or avatar-generation features. Protect shipped assets from extraction. Content is locked to purchase account; §5 provides Workgroup Accounts for multi-seat studios rather than a published numeric per-asset seat cap. A contractor can deliver finished projects, but original CC/iClone project access can require the client's own content rights. This report does not treat “Standard” as permission to build an in-game avatar generator. [EULA](https://www.reallusion.com/Content/EULA/EULA.htm).

**ActorCore versus CC outfit editing.** ActorCore delivers optimized prebuilt actors. Official character specification says ActorSCAN/ActorBUILD appearance customization is limited to materials in that pipeline; full body/outfit changes belong in their CC high-resolution assets. Do not buy a one-mesh scan assuming a separable dressed body underneath. [Character specifications](https://www.reallusion.com/content/characterspec/). The official Unity Auto Setup currently lists Unity 2022.3–6.3 and URP/HDRP but also **Windows-only** requirements: do not confuse direct browser FBX availability on Mac with a guaranteed Mac Auto Setup plugin. Manual URP material setup may be needed. [Auto Setup download/requirements](https://www.reallusion.com/auto-setup/unity/download.html).

**Rocketbox wardrobe inspection result.** Official published Fig.10 shows business/casual/occupational people; modern construction/firefighter/uniformed examples do not verify 1920s costume accuracy. The official paper confirms integrated clothing and describes photographic texture creation and LOD counts. A secondary inventory lists business (4 female/7 male), party (2 female), police (1 female/7 male), but exact repository filenames/costume cuts were inaccessible in this browsing environment; **do not claim seven period cops or two flappers**. [Official authors' paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/virtual-reality/articles/10.3389/frvir.2020.561558/full), [paper PDF](https://vhil.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj29011/files/media/file/gonzales-franco-fvr-rocketbox.pdf), [secondary catalogue count only](https://upcommons.upc.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/bf143f28-d6a7-4c2f-91bc-927ca9200ebe/content). **Mixamo evidence.** [Adobe FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html) verifies free commercial games; [Adobe rigging manual](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/help/mixamo-rigging-animation.html) verifies browser workflow and rigged FBX input. ### Planning estimate, not a seller quote

For **4–6 complete semi-realistic 1920s outfits on supplied bodies/skeletons**, reserve **$300–600 per outfit**: **$1,200–2,400 for four**, **$1,800–3,600 for six**, plus **25% contingency** → **$1,500–4,500 total** across the four-to-six-outfit range. This is our estimate derived from the garment, accessory and rigging prices above; none of the sellers promised this package. It assumes reuse of bodies, heads, hair and suitable shoes, baked bead/seam detail, ordinary hand-keyed or retargeted animation, and no cinematic cloth simulation. Elaborate bead geometry, extra body sizes, hero hair/face work, custom instruments and musician animation are additional scope.

At the cheap end, $100–200 clothes-only listings suggest a **$400–1,200 mesh/texturing subtotal** for four to six outfits, but their missing rigging/LODs/Unity testing make that a misleading final budget. Start with **one $300–600 complete pilot**; expand only after it deforms and shades well in the actual game.

### Concrete outfit brief

Commission (1) a dropped-waist flapper/beaded evening dress, (2) day dress plus cloche, (3) waitress dress plus apron, and (4) cigarette-girl variant plus tray/strap. For six outfits, add (5) a singer's evening variant and (6) the chosen city's police coat/cap uniform. A waistcoat/shirt/trouser set can serve bar staff and musicians if that is a greater gap than the sixth item.

Supply the exact purchased base and skeleton. Require preserved bone names/rest pose, fitted and weighted separate garment meshes, FBX plus `.blend`, PBR maps and editable sources, an agreed triangle/material budget with two reduced LODs, and a Unity 6.3 URP 17 sample prefab. Agree acceptance poses: walk, sit on chair/bar stool, arms up, pour, carry tray, and Charleston turns. For skirts, agree the use of extra skirt bones/cloth and prevent body penetration in those poses. Humanoid mapping retargets the body animation; it does not make unrelated garments fit automatically. These are proposed requirements, not capabilities verified for every listing.

Commission rights should explicitly cover modification, commercial game embedding/distribution, trailers and work by the team's contractors, with disclosure of any third-party meshes/textures. [Fiverr's official commercial-use guidance](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/360011569298--For-Commercial-Use-license-details) makes seller-specific exceptions material; [Upwork's official ownership guidance](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211063608-How-Upwork-handles-confidentiality-and-NDAs) assigns newly created work after full payment unless otherwise agreed. These do not erase restrictions on an existing licensed base.

## 9. Traps to avoid

1. **A dressed scan is not necessarily a reusable nude body.** Rocketbox clothes are integrated, and ActorSCAN is often one mesh. Confirm hidden geometry or use a full editable base before commissioning wardrobe swaps.
2. **“Rigged”, “FBX”, “Humanoid” and “facial rig” are different claims.** A Maya facial controller may not ship as Unity blendshapes. A Humanoid skeleton retargets animation; it does not fit skirts or transfer clothes automatically. All untested mappings remain flagged above.
3. **Fab storefront presence does not establish the requested licence/files.** Require a Standard licence and actual downloadable FBX. A MetaHuman-only listing fails the requested file filter even if its underlying rights allow other engines. Reference-Only delivers no source. The conditional leads above must not be treated as approved purchases.
4. **Do not buy the cheapest ArtStation tier blindly.** The bouncer's cheaper tier has no textures; ordinary Standard stock licences cap sales/views. Extended stock-asset rights differ from single-seat tool/tutorial licences. [ArtStation EUA](https://www.artstation.com/marketplace-product-eula).
5. **CGTrader subscription downloads differ from one-time purchases.** Its terms restrict unfinished projects after subscription expiry. Individual Royalty Free purchases are the simpler route for a game still in development. [CGTrader terms](https://www.cgtrader.com/pages/terms-and-conditions).
6. **Archviz “royalty free” is not a universal game licence.** AXYZ/metropoly needs extended game rights; Humano3D people need a custom licence; free 3DPeople Vivian is noncommercial evaluation. The £35.99 Flapper Cut hair needs the £285.99 commercial-project tier to ship. Source-specific terms are linked beside each entry.
7. **Reject editorial-only, noncommercial and legally Unreal-only assets for this project.** Independently check CC licences on free Sketchfab downloads and Gumroad seller terms. A famous gangland character, ripped game model or branded uniform is not cleared merely because someone uploaded it.
8. **Cheap “game-ready” metadata can be wrong.** The flapper outfit reports one polygon; a waitress reports 104 polygons and 646,000 vertices. Ask for actual LOD0 triangles, wireframe and material counts before relying on performance claims. Posed/4D people are not automatically skeletal NPCs.
9. **ActorCore is not Character Creator.** Direct actor FBX export can be used on Mac; CC wardrobe assembly still needs Windows, and Reallusion's current Unity Auto Setup page also lists Windows. Do not assume the browser-export route brings a Mac material importer with it. The newer Standard EULA supersedes old unlimited-character Extended advice, while generator/content-resale restrictions remain.
10. **Period fit still needs art direction.** A British late-Victorian policeman is not automatically a Chicago Prohibition cop; a modern gown is not automatically a flapper dress. Choose city/year before approving police insignia and cuts. Historical men are easier to source than women, and jazz performers still need instruments and performance animation.

## 10. Procurement order

First run an unpaid/free technical sample with Rocketbox, MPFB or Renderpeople Eric: Humanoid import, face close-up, one seated animation and URP materials. Then pay for **one complete period-woman outfit pilot**. The paid content source cost can be as low as **$44 for flapper + cloche**, but artist work is the real budget. After that pilot passes, expand to four women/staff variants and the city-appropriate police outfit. Keep invoices, the selected licence tier and a copy of the applicable terms with the asset records. No purchases or outreach were made during this research.
