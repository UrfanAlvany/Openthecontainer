# Commercial Performance & Player Reception of Incremental / "Reveal-Sell-Upgrade" Loop Games on Steam (as of 30 Sept 2026)

> **Methodology caveat (read first):** In this research session, direct page fetches to store.steampowered.com, steamcommunity.com, pcgamer.com, kotaku.com, raijin.gg, notebookcheck.net and itch.io were all **blocked by the network egress proxy**. Every figure below therefore comes from **web-search result snippets/summaries** of those pages (the URLs cited are the pages the snippets came from), not from a direct read of the page. Treat all numbers as "reported by search index at ~30 Sept 2026", i.e. medium confidence. Review counts move daily. Sales/revenue numbers are **third-party estimates** (Raijin, VG Insights/Sensor Tower, Gamalytic, Games-Stats, SteamSpy, Steam Revenue Calculator), never Valve data, unless marked "developer-reported".
>
> Gamalytic's own published error study: 77% of individual game estimates land within 30% of real sales, 98% within 50% — [Steam Page Analyzer summary of Gamalytic accuracy report](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/gamalytic-alternatives); [Gamalytic blog](https://gamalytic.com/blog/how-to-accurately-estimate-steam-sales).

## Which games made real money, how much, and why?

### Takeaway
Of the listed games, the clear commercial hits are Cookie Clicker (~2.6–2.8M Steam copies, ~$8–22M gross depending on estimator), CloverPit (developer-reported 1M+ copies at $10), and Scritchy Scratchy (~335K Steam copies, ~$2M gross, plus a reported 750K+ units across PC and mobile). Dealer's Life 2 is a modest earner (~127K units, ~$1.1M). The 2026 "Needle in a Haystack" clones have earned almost nothing so far (e.g. ~1,000 copies for one). The viral original has not launched yet, and its value is in wishlists and attention (50M views, 23K playtesters).

- User's "$2–4M gross" for Scritchy Scratchy: the **Steam-only** estimate (~$2M, Raijin) sits at the **bottom** of that range. $3–4M is only plausible once mobile and Switch 2 revenue and the reported 750K cross-platform units are included, and that total is unverified. The Boxleiter method at ~30–60 sales per review gives 15,131 reviews → ~450K–900K copies, higher than Raijin. The estimate range is wide.
- Why the winners won: (1) a **very low price** ($4.99–$10) that turns impulse buys into word of mouth; (2) a **tactile, legible reveal moment** (scratching, slot reel, cookie click) that makes good video clips; (3) **gambling fantasy without real money** (Kotaku's headline frames Scritchy as "lotto tickets without feeling terrible"); (4) cross-platform or mobile follow-through (Scritchy, Dealer's Life).
- Clones that copy only the *theme* (haystack, card shop) without the polish or the original's viral reach land at ~1K copies and Mixed reviews. Timing plus a meme brings visibility; it does not bring conversions.

## What features do reviews praise and criticise?

### Takeaway
Praise centres on **tactile, satisfying feedback** (hand-scratching, reveal moments), **fast early upgrade pacing into automation**, and **low price / short-session friendliness**. Complaints centre on **running out of content quickly**, **artificial padding / forced prestige resets**, and **repetitiveness after the first few hours**. For clone games, complaints are about **low quality and AI "slop"**.

### Cited Findings
**Scritchy Scratchy: praise**

- Upgrades raise ticket value, improve reward odds, speed up scratching and add automation. ## How long do players play, and how does content length relate to review score?

### Takeaway
The hits are **short** (median ~5–7 hours) and still score 90%+ because they are cheap. Endless incrementals (Cookie Clicker) score highest and sell longest. "Too short" is the most common complaint, but it does not sink reviews when price is ≤$10.

### Cited Findings
- **Scritchy Scratchy:** average playtime among reviewers ~**11 hours**. Main content **~5–7 hours**; all 34 achievements plus multiple prestiges **15–20 hours** — [Raijin playtime page / wiki (search summary)](https://raijin.gg/app/3948120/Scritchy_Scratchy/playtime); [Scritchy Scratchy wiki FAQ](https://scritchyscratchy.wiki/faq/). Review score 94%.
- **CloverPit:** **median ~6 hours, average >10 hours**; 90% positive — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/real-data-how-cloverpit-hit-750k)
- **Dealer's Life 2:** engaging for ~6 hours, then repetitive; 82% English positive — [Softpedia](https://www.softpedia.com/reviews/games/pc/dealer-039-s-life-2-review-534955.shtml); [stmstat](https://stmstat.com/app/1343670/reviews)
- **Nodebuster:** described on its store page as a "short, experimental incremental game" — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3107330/Nodebuster/)
- **Cookie Clicker:** effectively endless; 96% of 93K reviews and still 95% on recent reviews five years after its Steam launch — [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1454400/Cookie_Clicker)

### Inferences
- A rough rule from this sample: at $5–10, a 5–7 hour arc with a satisfying climax earns 90%+ even with "too short" complaints. At $14.99 (Dealer's Life 2), repetition after 6 hours costs more review points.
- Scritchy's "die four times before the end game" complaint shows the risk of stretching a short game with forced resets. Players tolerate shortness better than padding.

## Why so many haystack clones appeared at once, and what that says about copycat risk

### Takeaway
A single viral dev clip (~50M views, Aug 2026) about an absurd, easily described premise spawned at least ~8–10 Steam lookalikes and 14 Roblox copies within weeks. Many were built fast with generative-AI assets, riding search traffic for a game that was not yet buyable. The clones are commercially weak (~1K copies, Mixed reviews), but they pollute search and force the original to brand itself "the original". Viral reveal-loop concepts get cloned within days, so an unreleased viral game should ship or open a demo fast and own its name.

### Cited Findings
- Roblox creators made **14 copies within 48 hours** of the reveal; Needle In A Haystack Simulator faced **8 copycats on Steam before its own release** — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/the-steam-ripoff-market-is-so-bad-that-viral-game-needle-in-a-haystack-simulator-is-facing-8-copycats-before-release-and-has-slapped-original-on-itself-to-clarify/); [Roblox example](https://www.roblox.com/games/110954432401277/Needle-In-A-Haystack)

### Inferences
- Why all at once: (1) the premise can be explained in one line and built in days (hay particles, a shovel, a sell/upgrade shop); (2) the original was **unreleased**, so search demand for "needle in a haystack" had no purchasable answer; (3) generative-AI assets and Steam's low $100 Direct fee make speculative clones cheap; (4) incremental/upgrade scaffolding is commoditised.
- Trend signal: "absurd mundane task + incremental upgrades + co-op" is the 2026 meme format, following sorting and shelf games. Demand is real (23K playtesters from one clip), but the attention goes mostly to the original.
- Copycat risk for a new reveal-sell-upgrade game: expect clones within 1–3 weeks of any viral moment. Defences include a playable demo or playtest early, a distinctive and trademarkable name, visible "original" branding, and depth the clones can't copy quickly (e.g. Find The Needle's 300+ upgrade tree).
