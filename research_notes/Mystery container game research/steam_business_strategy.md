WebFetch was blocked by the network egress proxy for howtomarketagame.com, newsletter.gamediscover.co, partner.steamgames.com, gameworldobserver.com and gamesradar.com, so **every finding below comes from search-engine result summaries of those pages, not from full-page reads**. ## 1. Which genres/tags sell best for small indies on Steam (2024-2026)? Where do incremental/idle, roguelite, simulator, job-sim and co-op "friendslop" sit?

### Takeaway
2025 was the best year on record for indie hit rate (~3% of releases reached 1,000 reviews), and the three standout growth lanes for small teams were co-op "friendslop", job/business simulators, and idle/incremental games; roguelites (especially roguelike deckbuilders) are still big but increasingly saturated, with falling hit rates.

### Cited Findings
**Overall market**
- 20,282 games released on Steam in 2025; 608 reached 1,000+ user reviews (Zukowski, reported as "a bit of an indie golden age") — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/a-terrifying-20-282-games-were-released-on-steam-in-2025-and-just-608-managed-to-get-1-000-reviews-expert-finds-we-might-be-in-a-bit-of-an-indie-golden-age/); [How To Market A Game - golden age post](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/11/04/the-optimistic-case-that-indie-games-are-in-a-golden-age-right-now/)
- "A hair under 3% of all Steam releases" cleared 1,000 reviews in 2025, described as the highest success rate ever measured — [Spawn Point Marketing](https://spawnpointmarketing.substack.com/p/roguelite-fatigue-is-brewing-on-the)
- Indie games earned ~25% of Steam's ~$17.7B in 2025 (~$4.4B) (secondary aggregation of VG Insights/Alinea data) — [steampageanalyzer / search summary](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-revenue-data); earlier VG Insights data showed indies approaching AA/AAA revenue share on Steam — [Game World Observer, Oct 2024](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/10/16/indie-games-revenue-steam-vs-aaa-titles-vg-insights)
- The three best-selling *new* games on Steam in 2025 by units were indie titles priced under $20, 42M combined units, made by small teams — [AppMagic simulator report](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/steam-simulation-games-2026/?hl=en)

**Co-op "friendslop"**
- Term coined ~March 2025 for cheap, barebones online co-op games (often horror/comedy) driven by emergent group play — [Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/friendslop-peak-repo-fortnite-trend); [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025)
- Four co-op titles were in Steam's top 10 premium games by copies in 2025: R.E.P.O. (~18.5M), PEAK (~15.4M), Schedule I (~9.1M), RV There Yet? (~5.7M) (unit estimates) — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-top-selling-2025-friendslop-rpgs-sales-2000654157); [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/friendslop-steam-games-2025)
- Low price as a driver: Schedule I $20, R.E.P.O. $10, PEAK $8 — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-top-selling-2025-friendslop-rpgs-sales-2000654157)
- In Feb 2026 Next Fest, co-op games "punched above their weight"; "play with friends" energy drew disproportionate creator attention — [HTMAG Feb 2026 Next Fest analysis](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)

**Simulators / job simulators**
- Simulation surged in 2025 (Supermarket Simulator, Schedule I); players "particularly enjoying job simulators, especially blue-collar ones" — [HTMAG hit games of early 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/04/11/the-hit-games-of-early-2025/); [HTMAG Q2 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/08/04/2025-q2-games-that-are-selling/)
- Schedule I (TVGS): 9M+ copies, $172M+ revenue (estimate). Supermarket Simulator (Nokta Games, Turkey): >1 year in Early Access, full release June 2025, $45M+ revenue, 3.4M+ copies at $19.99 (estimates) — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/steam-simulation-games-2026/?hl=en)
- Trend is toward very specific fantasies (small businesses, everyday routines) rather than broad professions — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/steam-simulation-games-2026/?hl=en)

**Idle / incremental**
- 12 idle games reached 1,000 reviews by mid-2025 vs 16 in all of 2024; Zukowski noted "dozens of hit idle games" — [HTMAG golden age post (via search summary)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/11/04/the-optimistic-case-that-indie-games-are-in-a-golden-age-right-now/)
- Examples (estimates): Cast n Chill (Wombat Brawler, June 2025) ~$6.8M, 550K+ copies; The Farmer Was Replaced ~$6.8M, 960K+ copies; IdleOn (F2P) $22M+ in 2025 — [AppMagic idle games 2026](https://appmagic.rocks/research/idle-steam-games-2026)
- Number of idle games on Steam grew sharply over two years — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-50959)
- Desktop Defender (idle) entered Oct 2025 Next Fest with only ~200 wishlists (cited as a breakout example) — [HTMAG golden age post (search summary)](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/11/04/the-optimistic-case-that-indie-games-are-in-a-golden-age-right-now/)

**Roguelites**
- 212 roguelike deckbuilders shipped on Steam in 2025 (~1 every 41 hours); ~5.1% reached 1,000 reviews vs 6.71% the prior year — falling hit rate — [Spawn Point Marketing, "Peak Roguelite"](https://spawnpointmarketing.substack.com/p/roguelite-fatigue-is-brewing-on-the)
- Nevertheless 5.1% is still above the ~3% all-Steam average; breakout examples include CloverPit (100K+ wishlists pre-launch, 100K+ copies in 24 hours, 680K+ demo downloads during Next Fest) — [search summary citing opgamemarketing / CloverPit coverage](https://opgamemarketing.substack.com/p/making-and-marketing-your-indie-game); [Wikipedia: CloverPit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CloverPit)
- Big-name competition keeps arriving (Slay the Spire 2 EA March 2026, 574K peak CCU; Poncle's Vampire Crawlers April 2026) — [Recognizing Patterns](https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/the-roguelite-cull-hasnt-happened); [Spawn Point](https://spawnpointmarketing.substack.com/p/roguelite-fatigue-is-brewing-on-the)

### Inferences
- For a small team, idle/incremental is attractive: low content cost, strong "hook" in short sessions, Steam-native audience growing, and several $5-7M outcomes from tiny teams. Risk: supply is growing fast too.
- Combining genres that are each "working" (e.g., incremental + roguelite loop, or job-sim fantasy + incremental progression, or co-op) is a common pattern among 2025 hits, but that is an inference, not a measured stat.
- Roguelite deckbuilders still outperform the Steam average hit rate but the trend is down; differentiation of the hook matters more each year.

## 2. Wishlists: conversion to first-week sales, launch benchmarks, and the Boxleiter/review-multiplier method

### Takeaway
Expect roughly 0.10-0.17 first-week sales per launch wishlist (median; lower for >$10 games), Steam's "Popular Upcoming" visibility kicks in around ~7,000 wishlists gained in a compressed window, and total lifetime sales can be estimated at ~30x Steam reviews (range 20-60x+).

### Cited Findings
**Wishlist-to-first-week conversion (GameDiscoverCo)**
- 2024-2025 data: median first-week sales/launch wishlists = 0.17x for games with >10K launch wishlists; 0.15x for >25K wishlists — [GameDiscoverCo, "The state of Steam wishlist conversions: 2024-2025"](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions); [games.gg summary](https://games.gg/news/what-the-data-says-about-wishlist-conversion/)
- Games priced over $10 at launch: median drops to 0.10x — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)
- Seasonal: conversion spikes in February and August; December (Winter Sale) converts poorly. Removing adult games moves the median only from 0.15x to 0.14x — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)
- Earlier cut (games Aug-Oct 2024 with >=5,000 launch wishlists): median 10.5%; only ~10% of new games convert at a high ratio — [Game World Observer on GameDiscoverCo](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/12/06/wishlist-to-sales-ratio-steam-gamediscoverco-benchmark); [GameDiscoverCo "new wishlists to first week sales expectations"](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-the-new-wishlists-to-first)
- Conversion by eventual revenue tier (search summary of HTMAG benchmarks page; likely Gamalytic-sourced lifetime-ish conversion): ~$1K-revenue games 11.93%, $10K 20.49%, $100K 25.35%, $1M 27.08% — [HTMAG Benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/); see also [Gamalytic wishlist/sales ratio blog](https://gamalytic.com/blog/exploring-steam-wishlist-sale-ratio)
- Wishlists don't go stale: in Zukowski's Song of Iron analysis, early (Mar 2020) and late (Summer 2021) wishlisters converted at statistically identical rates — [HTMAG "Do wishlists get old?"](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/01/27/do-wishlists-get-old/) (via search summary)

**Wishlist benchmarks**
- ~7,000 wishlists accumulated within a compressed timeframe is roughly the threshold for appearing on Steam's Popular Upcoming list — [HTMAG benchmarks (search summary)](https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/)
- Zukowski "Gold" tier launch: ~30,000-50,000 wishlists at launch — [HTMAG, "How many wishlists should I have when I launch"](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/09/26/how-many-wishlists-should-i-have-when-i-launch-my-game/)
- Coming-soon page first-period wishlists by tier: Bronze 23-68, Silver 79-441, Gold 134-1,575, Diamond 426-4,275 — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2021/12/12/how-many-wishlists-can-you-expect-when-you-launch-your-games-coming-soon-steam-page/)
- Distribution: ~66% of new games launch with <10,000 wishlists; ~6% hit 100,000+ pre-launch — [search summary citing opgamemarketing](https://opgamemarketing.substack.com/p/making-and-marketing-your-indie-game) (secondary)
- Post-launch Discovery Queue drives nearly half of page visits in the first two weeks; most games get ~25,000-35,000 Day-1 impressions — [HTMAG, post-launch Discovery Queue](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/02/19/what-is-the-post-launch-discovery-queue/)
- Demo effect case study: one game went from 7,000 to 42,000 wishlists attributed to its demo — [HTMAG "The demo effect"](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/08/26/the-demo-effect-from-7000-wishlists-to-42000/)

**Boxleiter / reviews multiplier**
- Boxleiter number = copies sold per Steam review. Karl Kontus (GameDiscoverCo) study of 10,000+ games: recent releases cluster around ~30x; working range 20-40x — [GameDiscoverCo "Steam sales estimates: why game popularity matters"](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-sales-estimates-why-game-popularity); [steampageanalyzer explainer (secondary)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/boxleiter-method-explained)
- Multiplier rises with popularity: games with 1,000-10,000 reviews averaged ~52.8 sales/review vs ~36x for games under 100 reviews — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-sales-estimates-why-game-popularity)
- GameDiscoverCo caveat: estimates "can regularly be 50% or more different", up for viral/long-played games, down for pre-order/fan-first games; within one 50-game cohort day-1 multipliers ranged 10x-224x — [GameDiscoverCo via search summary](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/steam-sales-estimates-why-game-popularity)
- Tools: [Boxleiter Sales Estimator](https://steam.polybench.net/boxleiter/)

### Inferences
- Worked example (estimate): 10,000 launch wishlists x 0.10-0.17 = ~1,000-1,700 first-week units. At a $7.99 price and ~$4-4.50 net per unit (see Q5), that is roughly $4K-$7.5K net in week one.
- For planning, a small team should target 10K+ launch wishlists as a minimum viable bar and 30K+ for a "Gold" launch; a cheap (<$10) incremental game should convert at the higher end of medians because price dampens conversion.
- 1,000 reviews x ~30x ≈ 30,000 lifetime units, a useful "success" definition.

- No genre-specific conversion rates for incremental games found.

## 3. Demo strategy: web demo vs Steam demo, separate demo page, demo length, Next Fest timing, top-demo traits, Steam Playtest

### Takeaway
Release a Steam demo well before Next Fest (1+ month ahead yields ~2.5x the wishlists), enter Next Fest with as many wishlists as possible (pre-fest wishlists are the strongest predictor, r≈0.83), design for streamability, and consider itch.io/web builds or Steam Playtest earlier to validate and build wishlists before "spending" the one-time Steam demo notification.

### Cited Findings
**Next Fest dates**
- October 2026: Oct 19-26, 2026 (10:00 PDT); registration closed Aug 31, 2026 — [Steamworks Next Fest Oct 2026](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/2026october); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/events/steam-next-fest-october-2026/)
- February 2027: Feb 22 - Mar 1, 2027; register by ~Jan 10, 2027 — [Steamworks Feb 2027](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/feb_2027); [presskit.gg (secondary)](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/next-fest-scheduling-registration)
- June 2027: June 14-21, 2027; register by ~Apr 25, 2027 — [Steamworks June 2027](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest/june_2027)
- October 2027: not yet announced as of research date; pattern suggests mid/late October with registration closing ~start of September (inference by secondary source) — [presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/next-fest-scheduling-registration); Valve's 2027 schedule announcement — [Steamworks Development announcement](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steamworks/announcements/detail/668369251018480125)
- Demo submission: submit for review >=5 weeks before the fest to release before Press Preview; >=3 weeks before to release before fest start — [Steamworks Next Fest docs via search summary](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)
- Rule: a game can participate in Next Fest only once (general Steamworks rule; not re-verified this session — see Gaps).

**Next Fest benchmarks and what winners share (Zukowski)**
- Feb 2026 Next Fest: 3,500+ demos (3,449 entries), +19% vs Oct 2025, +51% vs Feb 2025 — [HTMAG Feb 2026 analysis](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- Feb 2026 wishlists added during the fest: median 806; 70th pct 1,839; 95th pct 13,461; top single game 57,074; widest median-to-top gap ever — [HTMAG Feb 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- No Diamond-tier game entered with <3,000 wishlists; most had 10,000+. "Next Fest mostly amplifies momentum a game already has" — [HTMAG Feb 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- Pre-fest wishlist count was the strongest correlate of Next Fest wishlists (Spearman r = 0.825) — [HTMAG Next Fest benchmarks, Mar 2025](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)
- Demos out >1 month before the fest earned ~2.5x the wishlists of demos launched during the fest — [HTMAG Feb 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- 46% of Feb 2026 entries were localized to Simplified Chinese (2nd most common language) — [HTMAG Feb 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/)
- June 2026 Next Fest reportedly had 4,200+ demos (record) — [tech-insider (secondary)](https://tech-insider.org/steam-next-fest-june-2026/); commentary that community-driven games won and AI-disclosed games underperformed — [Recognizing Patterns](https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/steam-next-fest-june-2026-communities)
- Top earners did significantly more marketing beforehand (creator outreach) and had demos that streamers could play and that brought streamers' audiences — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/want-to-know-the-best-way-to-gain-attention-on-steam-add-a-demo-); [ziva.sh summary (secondary)](https://ziva.sh/blogs/steam-next-fest-2026)
- Creator outreach recommended ~1 week before the festival — [secondary search summary](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/steam-next-fest-guide)

**Demo length**
- Median measured demo playtime ~14 minutes even though most devs designed ~30-minute demos; top Next Fest wishlist earners had median playtimes of 80 minutes to 3 hours (one 10-minute outlier) — [steampageanalyzer "How long should a Steam demo be" (secondary, citing Zukowski data)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-long-should-a-steam-demo-be)
- 68-88% of wishlists during fests come from people who never play the demo — the store page does most of the work (secondary) — [strayspark / search summary](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/steam-next-fest-demo-optimization-wishlists)

**Separate demo page**
- Steam (July 2024 update) lets devs enable a separate store page for the demo (own description, screenshots, trailer, and its own reviews); republish the base game so they are linked — [HTMAG "What Steam's big demo update means"](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/07/31/what-steams-big-demo-update-means-for-your-marketing-strategy/); [ResetEra thread](https://www.resetera.com/threads/steam-has-updated-how-they-handle-demos.937221/)

**Web/itch.io demo vs Steam demo**
- itch.io plays convert to Steam wishlists less efficiently than a Steam demo, but launching first on itch.io lets you experiment and build wishlists before the Steam demo launch, because a Steam demo release triggers a one-time wishlist notification — [HTMAG demo update](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/07/31/what-steams-big-demo-update-means-for-your-marketing-strategy/)
- Case studies of itch.io-to-Steam transitions (Zukowski) — [HTMAG "Can itch.io success translate to Steam success?"](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/05/22/more-games-that-made-the-itch-io-to-steam-transition/)
- Example: Gambler's Table devlog on itch.io reported 0 to 10,000 Steam wishlists in three weeks — [itch.io devlog](https://greenpixels.itch.io/gamblers-table/devlog/945763/wishlists-0-to-10000-in-just-three-weeks-)
- ~75% of itch.io traffic reportedly comes from browsing popular tags; itch.io has an active web "Incremental" tag — [search summary](https://itch.io/games/platform-web/tag-incremental) (secondary)

**Steam Playtest**
- Playtest runs on a separate associated appID, not tied to main game reviews, wishlists or store presence — [Game Launch Guide (secondary, citing Valve)](https://www.gamelaunchguide.com/blog/steam-playtest-launch-risk-filter/)
- Playtests are used as "marketing beats"; tinyBuild used Playtests plus demos to generate 3M+ wishlists across its portfolio in 2024 — [opgamemarketing substack](https://opgamemarketing.substack.com/p/making-and-marketing-your-indie-game)

### Inferences
- Recommended sequence for this team (inference): itch.io/web prototype -> Steam page up -> Steam Playtest (optional, for signups/feedback) -> Steam demo live 1-3 months before a Next Fest -> Next Fest (Feb or June 2027 are realistic targets; Oct 2026 registration already closed) -> launch 1-3 months after the fest.
- For an incremental game, demo length should allow a meaningful progression arc ("one more upgrade") yet cap before the full loop; top demos run long (80 min-3 h) because players keep playing, which suits the genre.
- Aim to enter Next Fest with 3,000-10,000+ wishlists.

### Gaps
- Could not verify Steamworks' exact wording on the "one Next Fest per game" rule or whether a demo shown before a fest is still eligible (policies change; check Steamworks directly).
- No quantitative comparison of browser-demo vs Steam-demo wishlist yields found.

## 4. Pricing for incremental/short-loop games ($3-$15), regional pricing, launch discounts

### Takeaway
Sub-$10 pricing is the norm for hits in this space (PEAK $8, R.E.P.O. $10) and conversion is materially better below $10 (median 0.10x above $10 vs ~0.15-0.17x overall); use Valve's suggested regional pricing from day one, and a 10-20% launch discount is standard.

### Cited Findings
- Games >$10 at launch: median wishlist conversion 0.10x vs 0.15-0.17x overall — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)
- 2025 breakouts were cheap: PEAK $8, R.E.P.O. $10, Schedule I $20 — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-top-selling-2025-friendslop-rpgs-sales-2000654157)
- Median indie price on Steam reported at ~$10.99-$15.64; $9.99 vs $10.00 described as a key psychological threshold (secondary) — [steampageanalyzer pricing guide](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-pricing-strategy)
- Regional pricing examples as % of US price (secondary, based on Valve suggestions): Argentina ~25%, Turkey ~30%, India ~35%, CIS ~40%, SE Asia ~45%, Brazil ~55%, China ~65%, UK/EU/Canada/Korea ~95%, Japan 100%, Australia ~105% — [steampageanalyzer regional pricing](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-regional-pricing-guide). Note: Valve moved Argentina/Turkey to USD pricing in 2023, so local-currency-era percentages may differ.
- Launch discount: 10-15% common for strong-wishlist launches; claim that discounts of 20%+ trigger wishlist "on sale" notification emails (secondary; Valve always sends a launch email to wishlisters regardless of discount) — [steampageanalyzer discount strategy](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-discount-strategy)

### Inferences
- For a small incremental game: $4.99-$9.99 is the sweet spot (inference from the above). $6.99-$7.99 with a 10-20% launch discount aligns with genre comps (e.g., Cast n Chill ~$6.8M on 550K copies implies ~$12 avg gross per copy; The Farmer Was Replaced ~$7 per copy — derived estimates from AppMagic figures).
- Keep price <$10 to avoid the conversion penalty and allow cheap impulse/gift purchases among friend groups.

### Gaps
- No incremental-genre-specific price elasticity data found.
- Did not verify current Valve rule on minimum discount thresholds and discount cooldowns (Valve rules previously: launch discount allowed, then 30-day cooldown between discounts; not re-verified).

## 5. Early Access vs full release for this genre

### Takeaway
Early Access is now common and not stigmatized for sims/roguelites (Supermarket Simulator spent >1 year in EA), but no comparative dataset for EA vs 1.0 outcomes in incremental games was found; for a short, content-light incremental game a full 1.0 launch is likely simpler.

### Cited Findings
- Supermarket Simulator spent over a year in Early Access before full release (June 2025) and made $45M+ (estimate) — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/steam-simulation-games-2026/?hl=en)
- Slay the Spire 2 launched Early Access March 5, 2026 at $24.99, peaking at 574,638 concurrent players — [Recognizing Patterns](https://recognizingpatterns.substack.com/p/the-roguelite-cull-hasnt-happened)
- Commentary that the EA label has shifted from "buyer beware" to "get in early" — [Medium research piece (low confidence)](https://medium.com/@research_86150/steam-in-2025-the-year-indie-games-ate-the-industry-alive-666abf04551f)

### Inferences
- EA's main value is a second "launch" (1.0 gets fresh visibility) and a revenue stream during development; its cost is splitting momentum and needing sustained updates. For a small incremental game with a finite content arc (a few hours), full release is likely cleaner; EA suits long-tail sims/roguelites with systemic content.

### Gaps
- No quantitative EA-vs-1.0 comparison (GameDiscoverCo/HTMAG) could be retrieved.

## 6. Revenue split and costs: 30% cut, refunds, VAT, $100 fee, realistic net per copy

### Takeaway
Plan on keeping roughly 50-57% of the headline US price per copy after VAT/sales tax, refunds, and Valve's 30% (before income tax, publisher shares or payment of team).

### Cited Findings
- Valve's cut: 30% up to $10M; 25% on $10M-$50M; 20% above $50M — [steampageanalyzer (secondary, consistent with Valve's 2018 policy)](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-revenue-share-explained)
- Steam Direct fee: $100 per product, non-refundable but recouped once the product reaches $1,000 Adjusted Gross Revenue — [Steamworks Steam Direct Fee docs](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee)
- Estimates that ~40% of Steam games never recoup the fee; over 5,000 games released in a year didn't reach the threshold — [fungies / search summary (secondary)](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained/); [Yahoo Tech](https://tech.yahoo.com/gaming/articles/over-5-000-games-released-152604830.html)
- Refund policy: any reason within 14 days of purchase and <2 hours playtime — [Steam Refunds](https://store.steampowered.com/steam_refunds/)
- Typical indie refund rate 10-15%, higher for short games (secondary) — [steampageanalyzer fee calculator](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/tools/steam-fee-calculator)
- Valve's share is calculated on revenue net of refunds, VAT/sales tax and chargebacks — [presskit.gg revenue calculator (secondary)](https://presskit.gg/tools/revenue-calculator)
- Worked example: $20 sale -> ~$17 after ~15% blended VAT -> ~$15 after 12% refunds -> ~$10.50 after 30% (~52% of list) — [search summary of steampageanalyzer / immutable guides (secondary)](https://www.immutable.com/guides/how-much-does-steam-take); Immutable headline: "You keep 35-57%, not 70%" — [Immutable](https://www.immutable.com/guides/how-much-does-steam-take)

### Inferences

- Short incremental games risk higher refunds if players can "finish" or see the whole loop in under 2 hours; ensure core content exceeds 2 hours.

## 7. Timeline: when to open the Steam page; capsule art; trailer best practices

### Takeaway
Put the Coming Soon page up 6-12 months before launch (as soon as the art style is final and there's a 30-second gameplay clip); capsule art is the highest-leverage asset and must read at tiny sizes; trailers should show gameplay within the first ~5 seconds.

### Cited Findings
- Valve data: games whose coming-soon page went up at least 6 months pre-launch had ~300% more sales than the average game whose page went up ~30 days before (via Zukowski) — [HTMAG 60 Marketing Mistakes ebook](https://howtomarketagame.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Zukowski_60MistakesEbookV1.pdf); [steampagecheck summary (secondary)](https://steampagecheck.com/coming-soon.html)
- Typical recommendation: publish Coming Soon page 6-12 months before launch — [presskit.gg marketing timeline (secondary)](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/indie-game-marketing-timeline)

## 8. Realistic outcomes: median indie revenue vs top 10%, and what separates them

### Takeaway
The distribution is extremely skewed: most Steam releases earn under ~$1K-$5K gross; ~10% exceed ~$50K; roughly 3% reach 1,000 reviews (~30K units); ~0.5% cross $1M. The separators are pre-launch wishlist momentum, a market-validated genre hook, demo/creator streamability, and price.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 distribution (secondary aggregation of VG Insights/Alinea data): ~66% of games earned <$1,000; ~90% earned <$50,000; ~0.5% crossed $1M; median indie game ~$249 gross in 2025 — [steampageanalyzer indie revenue data](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-revenue-data); [voxbooster stats (secondary)](https://voxbooster.com/blog/indie-game-statistics-2026/)
- Conflicting: same site also headlines median indie *lifetime* gross at $5K-$15K — [steampageanalyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/indie-game-revenue-data). These figures depend heavily on whether shovelware/asset flips are included; treat as a range, and prefer original VG Insights/Gamalytic reports.
- ~40% of games never recoup the $100 fee (<$1,000 AGR) (secondary) — [fungies](https://fungies.io/steam-revenue-share-explained/)
- Only ~3% of 2025 releases (608 of 20,282) reached 1,000 reviews — [GamesRadar/Zukowski](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/a-terrifying-20-282-games-were-released-on-steam-in-2025-and-just-608-managed-to-get-1-000-reviews-expert-finds-we-might-be-in-a-bit-of-an-indie-golden-age/)
- Separators documented: pre-fest wishlists (r=0.825 with Next Fest results) — [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/); demos out early (~2.5x) and extensive pre-fest marketing — [HTMAG Feb 2026](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/04/13/making-sense-of-the-february-2026-steam-next-fest/); conversion rises with revenue tier (11.9% at $1K vs 27% at $1M) — [HTMAG Benchmarks](https://howtomarketagame.com/benchmarks/); low price and co-op/streamability for breakouts — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-top-selling-2025-friendslop-rpgs-sales-2000654157)
- Tiny-team incremental outcomes in the top tier: Cast n Chill ~$6.8M; The Farmer Was Replaced ~$6.8M (estimates) — [AppMagic](https://appmagic.rocks/research/idle-steam-games-2026)

### Inferences
- Scenario bands for a small-team <$10 incremental game (label: estimates built from the above ratios, net ~$4/copy):
  - Weak: <3K launch wishlists -> ~300-500 first-week units, lifetime ~1-3K units -> ~$4K-$12K net.
  - Median-successful: ~10K wishlists -> ~1,000-1,700 first week, lifetime ~5-10K units (~150-300 reviews) -> ~$20K-$40K net.
  - Strong: ~30-50K wishlists ("Gold") -> ~4-8K first week, lifetime ~30K+ units (~1,000 reviews) -> ~$120K+ net.
  - Breakout: 100K+ wishlists and viral streaming -> hundreds of thousands of units, $1M+.
- The Steam page being up early, a demo in players' hands months before Next Fest, and a genre hook proven by recent comps appear to matter more than production value.
