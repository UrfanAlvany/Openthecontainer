# First-person shop / business simulators: how they play, why players love them, and where they quit

Researched 3 Oct 2026. Scope: Internet Cafe Simulator 1 and 2, Supermarket Simulator, TCG Card Shop Simulator, Schedule I, Gas Station Simulator, Dealer's Life 1 and 2 (added at the director's request), plus the strongest 2025–2026 shop-sim breakouts and some Mixed-review failures.

**How to read this file**
- **Review-sample analysis (my own work, label: "RS").** On 3 Oct 2026 I pulled English Steam reviews through Steam's public review API for 29 games. That gave up to about 300 negative and 200 positive reviews per game, sorted by "helpful in the last year" plus "most recent", with duplicates removed. "Median hours at review" is the reviewer's playtime when they wrote the review. It stands in for "when people make up their minds", **not** for average playtime. The keyword percentages are rough regex counts: an indication, not a measurement. Quoted reviews are from those pulls, and each one is linked to the game's public Steam review page.
- **Sales figures** are third-party estimates (AppMagic, Gamalytic, GameDiscoverCo, SteamSpy ranges) unless a developer stated them. SteamSpy owner ranges are stale or wrong for many 2025–26 titles (several 2026 games with 1,000–14,000 reviews show "0–20,000"). Where no estimate exists I give a **review-multiplier estimate** (Steam reviews × 30–60). That is a common rule of thumb, it is my own calculation, and it is labelled.
- Review scores and counts come from Steam's review summary API on 3 Oct 2026 (all languages unless stated).

## Key Question 0: Game-by-game breakdown (verbs, loops, customers, management vs hands-on, progression, threats, playtime, score, sales, team/engine, praise vs complaints)

### Takeaway
All the hits put one physical verb in your hands (stocking, ripping card packs, bagging product, pumping fuel, haggling) and add a business layer on top that keeps adding *new places, products or services*. They win early, at the "barely making rent" stage, and lose players at the point where hiring staff turns the job into logistics babysitting, or where unlocks stop changing what you do. Players quit around 2–10 hours in the weak games and around 25–50 hours in the strong ones (RS medians). The strong games keep players longer, but the complaints at the end are the same.

#### Internet Cafe Simulator 1 (2019) — the director's reference
- **Verbs and systems (store page):** set up and run the café; pay rent on your apartment and the shop; buy better gaming PCs; buy popular game licences to draw customers; rent new rooms to expand; buy crypto; "hire people to illegally give you a high score"; optional illegal work ("the price can be very heavy"); "Watch out and don't bankrupt!" — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1136160/)
- One terminal by the café entrance handles everything: "purchasing furniture and hiring staff to gambling and paying bills" — [Steam review, 30 up, 7h](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1136160/negativereviews/)
- Quit point: "there isn't much else to do. You can expand the café by adding more rooms, but more setups lead to more customers who block the exit"; crypto "only shows if the price has gone up or down from the day" — [Steam review, 9 up, 5h](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1136160/negativereviews/)
- Jank as comedy: guards are "complicit, allowing thieves access to your café in two out of three instances"; "If a chair is positioned too close to a table... your computer has likely toppled over" — [positive Steam review, 14 up, 30h](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1136160/positivereviews/)
- RS: 33% of negative reviews mention bugs and 13% mention theft or robbery; median hours at review 2.0 for negative reviews and 5.7 for positive — RS on [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1136160/negativereviews/)
- Mobile reach: Cheesecake Dev has about 30M Google Play installs across 2 apps; Internet Cafe Simulator has 10M+ — [AppBrain via search](https://www.appbrain.com/dev/Cheesecake+Dev/)
- Engine and team size: not found.

#### Supermarket Simulator (Nokta Games) — the template for the genre
- **Verbs (store page):** design the store; order stock on an in-game computer; unpack boxes into the storage room, then onto shelves, fridges and freezers; scan items; take cash and card; set prices against a live market ("Buy products when prices dip"); expand floor space; customise; fill online orders and deliver them yourself; visit local markets for deals; clean floors, empty trash, polish windows; cameras, alarms and guards against shoplifters; 4-player co-op — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2670630/)
- **Progression:** 26 product licences unlock 150+ products, each gated by store level, from Level 3 ($200, 6 products) up to Level 81 ($22k–34.6k) — [guide/wiki summaries via search](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3281216628). Pricing rule players share: price a little under the market price or it won't sell — [Gameleap guide via search](https://www.gameleap.com/articles/supermarket-simulator-beginner-s-guide-tips-on-how-to-run-a-successful-supermarket)
- Critic (7/10): the petty fun of paying change "in cents, with a tower that reaches up to the ceiling tiles"; "Watching your supermarket grow is seriously satisfying"; "customers are a little lacking in character"; complaints have little effect; "re-ordering can be a real rigmarole" — [GameSpew](https://www.gamespew.com/2025/11/supermarket-simulator-review/)
- **Mid/late-game frustration (Steam forum):** the player spends most of the time "ordering stuff and carrying them to storage" with "no time for break at all"; the expectation is that "when you build it big... owner work less and all load must go to staff", but staff can't carry the load. No developer reply in the thread — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/discussions/1/7607215340156983139/?l=english)
- Players describe it as fun for about 30 hours, then a "slog"; it becomes a "stockroom simulator"; even maxed restockers can't keep up, so the end-of-day restock takes 20–30 minutes; ordering can never be fully automated — [search summary of Steam discussions (secondary, not individually verified)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/discussions/0/4518884388506138759/)
- **Shoplifters:** they loiter at a shelf, then steal. A $100 bat makes them drop the goods and slows them down. Some players objected to the violence; the feature can be turned off — [search summary of wiki and Steam threads](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/discussions/0/600765572565727662/)
- **Monetisation backlash:** seven paid DLCs dated 6 Mar 2026 (Electronics, Clothing, Bakery, Ice Cream, Vending Machine, Essentials, Hardware) — [Steam search listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2670630/). Most-helpful negative review (289 up, 77h): "Fun for a few hours. Gets repetitive. Dev releases 6 paid DLCs that could've just been updates... the game 'art' is 100% AI with no disclosure" (the AI-art claim is the reviewer's, unverified) — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/negativereviews/). The Retro Rewind analysis says recent reviews fell to 68% over "greedy DLC tactics"; by 3 Oct 2026 they were back to 87% over 30 days — [Game Marketing substack](https://gamemarketing.substack.com/p/how-retro-rewind-sold-200000-copies); [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2670630/)
- A QoL patch won a lapsed player back: restockers can now be told to stop while you reorganise, "I went from finding the store management frustrating and tedious to actually enjoying spending time designing and organising my supermarket again" (291h) — [Steam positive reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/positivereviews/)
- **Launch and virality:** 33,000+ wishlists before launch; 3,000+ CCU on day one; peak 38,056 CCU (4 Mar 2024), above every PlayWay sim (Car Mechanic Simulator 2021 22.5k, House Flipper 2 18.1k); 4.2M Twitch hours, 141k peak viewers; free prologue, demo and Next Fest. Nokta has 4 employees and one main developer ("Cannias") — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/03/05/supermarket-simulator-viral-success-40k-ccu-turkish-devs). Later peak 51k CCU; "near-100% organic user acquisition"; 2.6M units, $27M gross — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/tcg-card-shop-simulator-the-second). 65,000+ reviews at 1.0 in June 2025 — [Games Press via search](https://www.gamespress.com/Supermarket-Simulator-Exits-Early-Access-and-Launches-V10-on-PC)
- RS: 24% of negatives mention DLC, 17% repetition or grind, 10% staff — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/negativereviews/)
- Engine: not confirmed in sources found.

#### TCG Card Shop Simulator (OPNeon, solo)
- **Verbs (store page):** design the shop; shelve card packs; open booster packs yourself; sell rare cards or keep them; host card-game sessions with a format and fee (keep the shop clean "or they might leave"); order products while following "popular trends"; expand space, unlock new card sets — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3070070/)
- **Developer's design intent:** "capture the excitement of opening a card pack and getting a rare card, and you get to decide whether to keep it for your own collection or sell it." Early on players are "quite hands-on in managing the shop and have less time and resources to open packs"; later "they are given a lot of tools to make the management part easier or more automated, so they get more time for the collecting part." Inspired by Recettear and Supermarket Simulator ("simple but pretty satisfying"). Unity plus Asset Store tools (A* Pathfinding Pro for customer paths) — [80.lv interview](https://80.lv/articles/solo-developer-on-creating-a-simulation-game-about-a-local-game-store)
- "My goal was never to create a hyper-strict or realistic simulator, but rather a cozy card shop owner's fantasy." Card grading came from player requests (0.60); the playable TCG was "the single largest community request" — [Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-originals/the-solo-dev-who-built-a-card-shop-game-and-watched-it-explode)
- **Progression:** Basic Card Box licence at shop level 3 ($150); Workbench and deck building at level 7; Ascension packs at 18 ($10k) and 32 ($15k); 9 workers unlock between levels 10 and 45 — [guides via search](https://tcg-card-shop-simulator.wiki/en/workers/worker-unlock-level/); [TreyEx 1.0 licences](https://www.treyexgaming.com/tcg-card-shop-simulator-1-0-shop-levels-and-licenses/)
- **1.0 (Sep 2026):** PS5, Switch and Switch 2 ports; "Tetramon" became a real playable TCG; about 2,000 new cards (4,436 total). Some reviews say the game cured their real pack-ripping habit. Dev: "I worked on it everyday and spent at least 90 hours every week for 6 months... I would have been really happy if it sold 100,000 copies in a year (it somehow sold that amount in just 3 days!)" — [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/tcg-card-shop-simulator-finally-hits-1-0-on-steam-after-4-million-players-96-percent-positive-reviews-and-2-years-in-early-access/)
- **Launch data:** about 400k sold in 10 days and 38k peak CCU; average playtime over 8h; Prologue demo hit 7,500 CCU; Supermarket Sim owners are 19.1× more likely to own it; the dev's earlier mobile game Idle Card Shop Tycoon had 1M+ downloads; "a broad influencer phenomenon" — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/tcg-card-shop-simulator-the-second)
- **Smelly customers:** some customers arrive smelly and drive others out without buying. You spray them with deodorant (~$63) or buy the Auto Scent M100 ($500) to spray them at the door. Some players call it a highlight; others call it a cheap shot at TCG players — [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/tcg-card-shop-simulator-what-to-do-smelly-customers/); [search summary of Steam/Reddit threads](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3070070/discussions/0/600775554275229403/)
- **Top positive review (538 up):** "I put up a tournament. Prize pool was 15'000$, Customers paid 31x600$ = 18'600$. I secretly snuck in the tournament... I won 1st place. 10/10, would rip-off my customers again" — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3070070/positivereviews/)
- **Complaints:** new 1.0 cards "locked behind a ridiculous progression wall. You have to grind all the way to Level 69 to 70" (46 up, 82h); "You find rare cards but in order to advance... you HAVE to sell them"; "employees you hire marked 'FAST'... walk so SLOW" (16 up); waited for the playable TCG, "Haven't seen new content in months" (18 up, 244h) — [Steam negative reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3070070/negativereviews/)
- "It's a very addicting game for how ugly it is... There's a large ceiling of progression so it keeps me going" — [Steam positive review](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3070070/positivereviews/)

#### Schedule I (TVGS) — solo-dev crime business sim
- **Verbs:** grow, cook and manufacture (weed, meth, cocaine, mushrooms); mix with corner-shop items (soda, paracetamol) to raise value and add comic effects; deliver on foot, skateboard or car; hire dealers, then employees (botanists, chemists, handlers/packagers, cleaners); buy properties and laundering fronts; avoid police (wanted system); fight the rival Benzies cartel; co-op — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schedule_I_(video_game)); [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3164500/)
- **Hands-on crafting is the point:** "Every step... from setting up dead drops, growing plants... and bagging it up into product, is hands-on. It's busywork, but chores don't feel like chores when you turn them into minigames." Upgraded stations swap in new minigames (a rotary packager with a timing game). Pricing per customer brings "a constant worry that I'm undervaluing my own product." "I'm not sure if the game will maintain the same charm once most of these tasks are automated... one day, you've gone corporate." — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/schedule-1-is-great-because-it-knows-crafting-sucks-unless-you-turn-everything-into-a-fun-minigame/)
- **Progression:** about 51 ranks (10 named ranks × 5 tiers, plus Kingpin), "Street Rat" through "Kingpin". Each tier unlocks seeds, ingredients, equipment and regions: Northtown at Street Rat I, Westville at Hoodlum I, Uptown (6th and last region) at Baron I. Kingpin tiers unlock nothing — [Game Rant / Shacknews via search](https://gamerant.com/schedule-1-ranks-unlocks/)
- Customer addiction sets how often customers buy and how much they pay — [Steam guide via search](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3475519606)
- **Sales and CCU:** all-time peak 459,075 CCU on 6 Apr 2025, 12 days after launch — [SteamDB via search](https://steamdb.info/app/3164500/charts/). Est. 8.9M copies / $153M gross — [Gamalytic via search](https://gamalytic.com/game/3164500); 9M+ / $172M+ — [AppMagic via search](https://appmagic.rocks/blog/steam-simulation-games-2026/?hl=en). Golden Joystick 2025 Breakthrough Award — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schedule_I_(video_game))
- **Team and roadmap:** solo developer; TVGS became a studio with a Sydney office and a second developer, planning about 4 staff — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/schedule-1-dev-announces-new-game-studio-reveals-2026-update-3300888/). In April 2025, 1.0 was "two to three years away"; the first major update went to a community vote between cartel, police raids and mushrooms — [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/schedule-1-final-release-is-three-years-away-according-to-its-developer-first-major-update-will-be-community-voted/)
- **Quit point (most-helpful negatives):** "The positive reviews for this game are people who havent reached the nonexistent endgame. The early loop is fun because youre constantly on the edge of your seat trying to make ends meet monetarily. After that its just meh. The only reason I even have 90 hours is because Ive replayed the beginning like 4 times" (48 up, 92h); "It became boring and repetitive, it feels like the target is to make more and more money for no reason. I'd rather have smaller ops with less NPC/employee management and an actual use of the money" (35 up, 24h); "no new and meaningful content added since the shrooms update came out in late 2025" (38 up) — [Steam negative reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/negativereviews/)
- **Automation feedback:** the curve "flattens out about halfway through the game, where things are kinda automated but I'm still doing too much transporting and manual correction"; storage limits (8 slots); handing cash to employees is tedious; players want a delivery employee; a "Reshelves" mod exists for auto-restocking — [Steam suggestions thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/discussions/0/830459500313982070/)
- A player wants more risk: "maybe it's risk? ... security upgrades or bribing the police otherwise you get raided" (1 up, 63h) — [Steam positive reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/positivereviews/)
- RS: negative reviewers had a median of 35.7h at review (the longest of any game sampled); 13% of negatives say "boring", 15% repetitive, 30% bugs (mostly multiplayer) — [Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/negativereviews/)

#### Gas Station Simulator (DRAGO, Poland)
- **Verbs (store page):** buy an abandoned desert gas station; clear debris; repair, paint and decorate; pump fuel; run the register; expand into toilets, shop, repair workshop and car wash; peak hours bring "serious time pressure"; impatient customers; hire and train employees when it becomes "just too much to handle for you alone" — [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1149620/)

#### Dealer's Life 1 and 2 (Abyte) — the haggling reference
- **Trade Engine (DL1 store page):** thousands of procedurally generated customers whose psychological traits "are reflected in their appearance". Each can counter-offer, accept, walk away, get angry at a ridiculous offer, or impose a final offer. "The goods' real value is never shown anywhere"; you estimate it with your Competence skill, with "No in-game guides". Skills improve fake-spotting and persuasion. Reputation drives random events (e.g. you scammed someone who "was later revealed to be a buddy of the local mafia") — [Steam store DL1](https://store.steampowered.com/app/982270/)
- **Design lineage:** "heavily inspired by... Papers, Please", plus TV pawn-shop and auction shows — [IndieDB / ModDB press kit via search](https://www.indiedb.com/games/dealers-life/presskit)
- **DL2 additions:** start in a home garage and grow to an empire; scammers with counterfeit items and contracts; hire employees; auctions; investments; a full story; 700+ collectible items in an Item Catalogue — [Steam store DL2](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1343670/). Four skills (charisma, competence, insight, luck). "With 8 or higher competence you don't have to worry about the fake items." Staff (bouncers, experts who spot fakes, and others) are rated 1–3 stars, with higher wages for higher stars — [GameGrin via search](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/dealers-life-2-review/)
- Critic: the haggling "balances revealed info and hunches well", but "After 5–6 hours, you'll just be bored of it" — [Softpedia via search summary](https://www.softpedia.com/reviews/games/pc/dealer-039-s-life-2-review-534955.shtml)
- **Quit point, DL1:** "after 3 hours or so you reach a point where you can hire any employee you need and you skip 90% of your customers without even bargaining because the items they bring are not worth it" (6 up, 2h); praise: "Buying low and selling high is addictive! Definitely a 'one more round' kind of game" — [Steam DL1 reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/982270/negativereviews/)
- **Quit point, DL2:** "You don't know an item's value. No worries. You don't need skill or employee. Buy it anyway... you will find another idiot who will pay 100k more" (139 up, 7h); "In the endgame, the gameplay degrades to set the price up when selling and down when buying... Praise: "'Yes.. yes I will buy your pair of funny Sun glasses for $20.' ... 'I'm gonna have to say $1000 on that pair'... 10/10 Would legally scam again" (18 up) — [Steam DL2 reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1343670/negativereviews/)

### Inferences
Loop structure, synthesised from the mechanics above (my framing):
- **30-second loop:** one physical, legible action with a sound or number payoff: scan and give change (Supermarket), rip a pack and see the rarity (TCG), fill a bag or hit the packager timing (Schedule I), stop the pump in the sweet spot (Gas Station), counter-offer and read the face (Dealer's Life), rewind a tape (Retro Rewind).
- **5-minute loop:** one business day or one delivery run. Retro Rewind's 6–8-minute day is the clearest data point. Supermarket players ask for longer days because the end-of-day restock overruns. The day ends on a profit tally and a choice of what to buy next.
- **1-hour loop:** a new licence, room, product line, region or employee. The hits make sure most hours bring something that changes *what you physically do* (a new service minigame, new drug chain, new region with new customers, tournaments, grading). Where an hour only brings *more of the same SKU volume*, the "repetitive" reviews start.
- Quit-point bands (RS medians at negative review): **2–7h** for the weaker or janky games (ICS1, Supermarket Together, Car Service Together, Bakery, Food Truck, Dealer's Life); **8–15h** for mid-tier (Gas Station, Cash Cleaner, Retro Rewind, Grocery, Megastore); **25–47h** for the top hits (Supermarket Sim, TCG, Schedule I, Car Dealer). The top hits don't avoid the late-game complaint; they push it 20–30 hours later.

### Gaps
- Engines unconfirmed for Supermarket Simulator, Internet Cafe Simulator 1/2 and Gas Station Simulator. Team sizes unknown for Cheesecake Dev and Abyte.
- No authoritative Steam sales figures for ICS1/ICS2 or Dealer's Life 1/2. The SteamSpy ranges and my review-multiplier figures are rough.
- True average or median playtime (HowLongToBeat, SteamDB) was not collected for most titles. The RS medians are a proxy for when people write a review.
- AppMagic's 2026 simulator article is JavaScript-rendered and could not be read in full. Its figures (Supermarket $45M/3.4M, Car Dealer $11.7M/658K, Storage Hunter $8M/547K, Megastore $1.8M/143K, Retro Rewind $8M/452K, and an unnamed "$18.5M / 1.1M in a few months" title) are taken from search snippets.

## Key Question 1: What makes running a small shop fun minute to minute, not just "number go up"?

### Takeaway
Minute to minute, the fun is **tactile, legible chores under light time pressure**. Every action is physical, finishes in seconds and has a visible result (a full shelf, a stack of change, a bagged product, a cleaner room). It sits inside a **precarious early economy** where each sale matters. Players repeatedly say the best part is the scrappy start ("on the edge of your seat trying to make ends meet"), and the second-best part is **watching the place physically transform**.

### Cited Findings
- Schedule I's hit status is credited to making every crafting step a hands-on minigame: "Nothing kills my buzz faster in a simulator than when most of the 'simulating' happens in menus. Schedule 1 is the total opposite"; "chores don't feel like chores when you turn them into minigames"; upgraded tools *change the minigame* rather than just speeding it up — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/schedule-1-is-great-because-it-knows-crafting-sucks-unless-you-turn-everything-into-a-fun-minigame/)
- "The early loop is fun because youre constantly on the edge of your seat trying to make ends meet monetarily. After that its just meh... Ive replayed the beginning like 4 times" — [Schedule I Steam review, 48 up, 92h](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/negativereviews/)
- A Supermarket Sim player's day-one story: "Stock shelves with your $200 – Realise you don't have enough so you only sell bread and flour... Get angry at customers for giving you a $100 bill for a $10 purchase – Forgot to set prices... lose money – Take out a loan" (88h) — [Steam positive reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/positivereviews/)
- "Filling the shelves is very satisfying, and the sound the register makes will soothe you"; it's for "pretending something in your life is at order for once" (154h) — [Supermarket Sim Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/positivereviews/)
- The Gas Station "single most satisfying thing" is "watching your station evolve visually across dozens of hours" — [Steam review, 48h](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1149620/positivereviews/)
- Retro Rewind's success is put down to nostalgia plus polish. Stock animates "from open boxes in a very satisfying manner" (summary of the author's description) — [Game Marketing substack](https://gamemarketing.substack.com/p/how-retro-rewind-sold-200000-copies)
- Cash Cleaner: "I love the physical feel of everything due to grabbing and throwing cash piles, boxes" — [Steam review, 28h](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2488370/positivereviews/); median playtime was three times the team's prediction — [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-cash-cleaner-simulator-hit-100k)
- Commentary on why: "Goals are straightforward and the progress is measurable"; repetitive tasks let the mind wander — [Mirkowski substack](https://adammirkowski.substack.com/p/zen-supermarkets-and-the-art-of-simulator). Job sims put you "in control of the job", unlike a real job; and the clean-up-a-mess satisfaction — [search summary of job-sim features (secondary)](https://filmstories.co.uk/features/job-simulators-find-magic-in-the-mundane/). Steam players especially like blue-collar job sims — [How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/04/11/the-hit-games-of-early-2025/)
- Players value the zoned-out state: "it let me slip into customer service mode and just shut my brain off" (Grocery Store Sim, 39h) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2961880/positivereviews/); Schedule I "allows me to completely zone out for hours" (180h) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/positivereviews/)
- Organising is a hobby inside the game: "i discovered i could organize the shelves. big mistake. i now know exactly where every product belongs" (79h) — [Grocery Store Sim review](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2961880/positivereviews/)
- TCG's dev framed it as a fantasy, not a simulation: "there's no way in real life you can rip cases of card packs at the corner of your own pitch-black card shop" — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/solo-developer-on-creating-a-simulation-game-about-a-local-game-store)
- When the verb has low fidelity, the game fails: Food Truck Simulator puts "a SINGLE slice of pepperoni on the pizza" for a pepperoni order (82 up) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1160920/negativereviews/); Bakery Simulator's minigames are "very disappointing and boring" (60 up, 1h) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1031120/negativereviews/)

### Inferences
- Five things carry the minute-to-minute fun: (1) **a physical verb with juicy feedback** (sound, animation, number); (2) **scarcity at the start**, so each sale or choice matters; (3) **visible transformation** of the space; (4) **a light clock** (day length, impatient customers) that creates mild pressure without real failure; (5) **mindless-but-tidy** states players can zone into.
- "Number go up" alone is what players *complain* about at the end ("You watch numbers go up. That's the whole game", Dealer's Life 2). The fun lives where the number is still small enough that the next purchase is a real decision.
- For the container game: the scrape/reveal is our "rip a pack" verb, and the early "can I afford the next container" tension is our Schedule I opening. The research says to protect both: keep the verb tactile even after upgrades, as Schedule I does by swapping minigames instead of deleting them, and keep money tight for longer than feels comfortable.

### Gaps
- There are no controlled studies or developer telemetry on which micro-actions players enjoy most. The evidence is reviews and critic commentary.
- No source measured how day length affects retention. Retro Rewind's 6–8-minute day is just one data point.

## Key Question 2: Which mechanics create stories and clips (customer AI comedy, thieves, chaos events, rare items)?

### Takeaway
Clips come from four places: **(1) getting to be a petty, unethical or violent owner** (overcharging, scamming, rigging tournaments, hitting shoplifters); **(2) customer jank and physics**; **(3) rare pulls and hidden combinations**; **(4) chaos events that break the routine** (thugs, bombs, smelly customers, saboteurs). Mischief the *player* chooses creates far more goodwill than threats the *game* imposes. Imposed threats get praised as "chaos" at low frequency and resented as "stress upon stress" when they're constant.

### Cited Findings
- **Player as petty villain:** TCG's most-helpful positive review (538 up) is the player rigging their own tournament to pocket the prize pool — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3070070/positivereviews/). Supermarket Sim: paying change "in cents, with a tower that reaches up to the ceiling tiles" — [GameSpew](https://www.gamespew.com/2025/11/supermarket-simulator-review/). Schedule I co-op lets players "bully my friends into doing the hard parts" (headline) — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/schedule-1-is-my-favourite-co-op-game-of-the-year-because-it-lets-me-bully-my-friends-into-doing-the-hard-parts-for-me/)
- **Thieves and shoplifters:** Supermarket Sim's thieves loiter, then steal; a $100 bat makes them drop the loot. ### Inferences
- The most-shared moments in these games are almost all **player-authored mischief or lucky finds**, not scripted events. A container game that lets players be a little greedy or petty (lowballing a buyer, flipping junk at a markup, a cheeky auction snipe) is following proven clip territory, and the honest-odds rule still holds.
- **Rare-item reveal moments** (TCG packs, Storage Hunter's rare finds, Schedule I combos) are the closest analogue to our reveal. Those games make rarity *readable at a glance* (rarity tiers, foils, named strains), which is what makes them clip well.
- Threats work as **occasional spice the player can answer with a funny tool** (bat, deodorant spray, water gun). They fail as **constant upkeep** (Waterpark).

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on which mechanics generate the most TikTok/YouTube clips. This is inferred from reviews and press.
- No direct evidence on how often ICS1/ICS2 chaos events fire, or how players rate their frequency.

## Key Question 3: How do the hits handle the shift from doing everything yourself to hiring staff? Does automation kill the fun?

### Takeaway
Hiring is the genre's main breaking point. **Broken staff** (slow, stuck, unable to keep up) turn the owner into a logistics babysitter and cause most mid-game quits. **Complete automation** removes the verb and leaves "nothing to do" (Schedule I, Dealer's Life). **No automation at all** feels like a treadmill (Waterpark, Supermarket's manual ordering). The best-received design (TCG's stated intent, Cash Cleaner) automates the *boring* verb so the player can spend more time on the *favourite* verb (opening packs, collecting), and keeps the player as the bottleneck for the exciting part.

### Cited Findings
- **TCG's explicit design:** early on, hands-on management with "less time and resources to open packs"; later, "tools to make the management part easier or more automated, so they get more time for the collecting part" — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/solo-developer-on-creating-a-simulation-game-about-a-local-game-store). Workers unlock gradually at levels 10–45 — [guide](https://tcg-card-shop-simulator.wiki/en/workers/worker-unlock-level/). Complaint: "FAST" workers "walk so SLOW" — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3070070/negativereviews/)
- **Supermarket Simulator:** players expect "owner work less and all load must go to staff", but restockers can't keep up and staff can't move goods from the street to storage — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/discussions/1/7607215340156983139/?l=english). "there's no way to completely automate stock ordering" — [search summary of reviews](https://www.gamespew.com/2025/11/supermarket-simulator-review/). A QoL fix to restocker behaviour brought a lapsed 291h player back — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/positivereviews/)
- **Supermarket Together:** "There is an upgrade to automate it but it is only unlocked at earliest in the midgame... I rushed it and got it around day 40" (33 up, 47h) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2709570/negativereviews/)
- **Schedule I:** "curve flattens out about halfway... kinda automated but I'm still doing too much transporting and manual correction" — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/discussions/0/830459500313982070/). DL2: with competence 8+, fakes stop mattering — [GameGrin via search](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/dealers-life-2-review/)

### Inferences
- In these reviews, players want automation to reach exactly the point where the *boring* verbs are delegated, and no further. The *signature* verb (rip packs, haggle, reveal) should stay the player's.
- The genre's worst-reviewed moment is **"I hired staff and now I babysit them"**: slow pathing, stuck NPCs, staff who can't use the tools you bought. If the container game adds crew or helpers, they must work reliably (simulated in `sim/`, no pathing jank in the web build) and should hand the player *more reveals*, not more chores.
- The Dealer's Life case is a warning for any appraisal or skill system: once an upgrade removes uncertainty (perfect fake detection), the skill game is over. Upgrades should *widen* the decision space (bigger containers, riskier lots, new item categories), not *solve* it.

### Gaps
- No developer postmortem gives retention curves before and after hiring unlocks.
- No source compares staff-AI quality across titles directly. The evidence is review sentiment.

## Key Question 4: Why did some copies of this formula fail (Mixed reviews)? What separates hits from "simulator slop"?

### Takeaway
The failures fail in the same few ways: **bugs and broken staff AI, abandonment (no updates or missed roadmaps), a weak or low-fidelity core verb, a 15-minute loop with no new verbs behind it, and greedy or misleading presentation (paid DLC, AI art, cloned brands)**. The hits share **a theme with built-in emotion** (nostalgia, collecting, crime, laundering money "literally"), **one excellent tactile verb**, **visible polish**, a **$15–20 price**, a **long demo or prologue**, and **creator-driven discovery**. The market is crowded (14 retail sims in one PC Gamer week), but the median shop sim still earns far more than the median Steam game.

### Cited Findings
- **Market scale:** of 107 "true 3D shop simulation games" since Jan 2020, the median net revenue was $24,185 against $749 for Steam overall, and 76.4% earned over $10k. Games priced $19.99+ cluster among the top earners. Longer demos correlate with higher revenue. Retro Rewind beat themed rivals (Record Shop Simulator, DVD Store Simulator) on "high quality art, good parody movies, and solid gameplay mechanics" — [Game Marketing substack](https://gamemarketing.substack.com/p/how-retro-rewind-sold-200000-copies)
- **The flood:** "Last week, 14 retail sims were released on Steam... you unpack boxes, operate checkouts, purchase and receive stock. They all kinda look the same. Many are seemingly set on the same eerily quiet street." AI-generated capsule art is "rife in this genre". PlayWay has 474 Steam products and a "centralized ideas machine" (Chris Carless quote). "If Lava Lamp Shop Simulator fails, maybe Tupperware Shop Simulator won't." Studios that put in real effort (Blood Pact, Caikemi) stand out — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-a-torrent-of-janky-retail-sims-continues-to-flood-steam-and-theres-no-end-in-sight/). PlayWay announces many pages and builds only those that gather wishlists — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2021/09/03/how-publisher-playway-strives-by-promoting-dozens-of-steam-games-that-might-never-come-out)
- **Player backlash:** discovery queues "FILLED with all these One week project slop trash shovelware Shop sims that are ALL the same"; "asset flip games people pay a week to make" — [Steam Universe group](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/steamuniverse/discussions/0/591772052728332551/)
- **Abandonment** is the top Mixed-review driver in RS: Fast Food Simulator 60% of negatives mention abandonment ("gets boring after 20–30 hour mark because no more updates", 94 up); Mall Simulator 44% ("the 'gameplay loop' lasts about 15 minutes before you realize there's nothing else to do", 87 up; "AI cloned copies of brands", 222 up); Motel Manager 22%; Grocery 19%; Bakery 14% (devs "will not be releasing any bug fixes or content updates") — Steam reviews for [Fast Food](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2916430/negativereviews/), [Mall](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3180150/negativereviews/), [Bakery](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1031120/negativereviews/)
- **Bugs:** Kebab Chefs 66% and Bakery Simulator 61% of negatives mention bugs (RS) — [Kebab](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1001270/negativereviews/), [Bakery](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1031120/negativereviews/). Cafe Owner Simulator (Mixed, 63.3% of 1,113): "It's not a bad little game. So why am I giving it a thumbs down? Simply because it's broken" (82 up) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1498140/negativereviews/)
- **Weak verb or long grind:** Pharmacy Simulator (Mixed, 58.8% of 257): "It gets repetitive within 20 minutes"; "serving over a hundred customers to get to Level 10 to take the first exam to get new meds" — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2930320/negativereviews/). Food Truck Simulator (DRAGO, Mixed 68.7%): inventory spoilage UI and the single pepperoni slice — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1160920/negativereviews/)
- **Drug Dealer Simulator 2** (Mixed, 69.8% of 5,938): "basically a running simulator... Critics: overcomplicated drug-making, too much narrative for a tycoon game — [search summary of reviews](https://game8.co/articles/reviews/drug-dealer-simulator-2-review)
- **Even hits drift down on monetisation and neglect:** ICS1 and ICS2 recent reviews are 58% and 60% (Mixed territory) against 73% all-time — [Steam ICS1](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1136160/), [ICS2](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1563180/). Gas Station's top negative is about paid DLC over fixes — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1149620/negativereviews/). - **What the hits did:** Supermarket Sim: free prologue, demo, Next Fest, streamer-driven, "near-100% organic" — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/03/05/supermarket-simulator-viral-success-40k-ccu-turkish-devs); [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/tcg-card-shop-simulator-the-second). - **Theme specificity can backfire when the systems are shallow:** Retro Rewind is a hit, yet "You can't set prices... virtually no management" (62 up) — [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3552140/negativereviews/)

### Inferences
- "Slop" in players' eyes = **same street, same box-stocking verb, AI art and cloned brands, no updates**. A theme alone does not differentiate. The hits pair the theme with **a verb that only that theme has** (rip packs, rewind tapes, launder bills, mix drugs, haggle over a fake Rolex).
- Abandonment is so heavily punished in reviews that a **visible, kept roadmap** works as a retention and review-score mechanic. Even the biggest hits (Schedule I, TCG) get their most-helpful negatives for content droughts.
- Paid DLC that looks like "updates split off" damages even 90%+ games. If we ever do DLC, it should be clearly new content (new port, new container class), never base-game features.

### Gaps
- No postmortem from a failed shop-sim developer was found. The failure reasons come from player reviews only.
- The share of shop sims that use AI art, and its measured effect on sales, is not quantified.

## Key Question 5 (director's focus): Progression ladders — does each step change HOW you play? Does the player's own skill improve? What risk or loss exists?

### Takeaway
The director's hunch is supported, with a condition. **Progression is the engine, but only when steps add new verbs, places, customers or threats.** Where steps only raise numbers (more SKUs, faster vehicle, 4× upgrade costs), reviews turn to "same thing over and over". **Player skill** is real early (pricing, layout, haggling, reading customers, routing) but usually gets **solved or automated away** (pricing formulas, competence 8+, skip 90% of customers). **Risk and loss** are mostly soft (lost sales, fines, theft, wages). The games players call most gripping (Schedule I's opening, Dealer's Life's early haggles, Storage Hunter's bids) are the ones where a bad decision costs you something real.

### Inferences
- **Ladder rule from this genre:** every rung that only multiplies numbers costs goodwill. Every rung that adds a *new place, new customer type, new verb or new threat* buys roughly another session of interest. Schedule I's regions and drug chains, and Gas Station's service minigames, are the models. Supermarket's licences and Storage Hunter's late upgrades are the cautionary cases.
- **The skill game dies when an upgrade or a formula solves it.** For the container game: player skill should live in *reading partial reveals* (shapes, glints, manifests) and *deciding what to bid or sell*, and upgrades should never fully reveal or auto-appraise. They should give *better information at a cost*, so the judgement call survives. This matches Rule 3 in CLAUDE.md (near-misses only from real partial reveals).
- **Risk:** the most-praised moments come from real but bounded loss (bad bids, fakes, police heat). Players complain both when there is too little risk (Schedule I late game, Supermarket "minimal consequences") and when there is too much (Waterpark). A container game naturally has the right kind of loss: you paid for a dud box. The research supports keeping that loss visible and survivable, not smoothing it away.
- **Late game:** every hit's late-game complaint is "money with nothing to spend it on". Prestige, collections and new ports and container classes (already in GAME_DESIGN.md) are exactly the money sinks these games lack.

### Gaps

- No primary data on Storage Hunter's bidding mechanics, the analogue most relevant to the container auction. A focused follow-up on Storage Hunter Simulator and Storage Wars-style auction games is recommended.
- Exact Supermarket licence costs vary between sources ($22,000 vs $34,600 at L81). Treat them as approximate.
