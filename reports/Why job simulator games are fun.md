# Every Level Must Change the Work

First-person job and shop simulators are fun for three reasons that stack. The first is **one physical verb that visibly changes the world within a second**: spray, stock, rip a pack, haggle. The second is **an early economy tight enough that every sale matters**. The third is **a ladder whose rungs add new verbs, places, customers or risks**. Players quit at predictable points, after about **2–7 hours in weak games and 25–47 hours in the hits**, and always for the same few reasons: money stops buying anything new, hired staff turn the owner into a babysitter, the last 1% of a job becomes a pixel hunt, or the developer stops updating. The director's instinct about Scritchy Scratchy and Dealer's Life is right, with one condition. "The next level" pulls players only when it changes *how* they play, and "getting better" counts only when it is the player's own skill. Dealer's Life is the failure case: players went from calling it a "one more round" game to "You watch numbers go up. That's the whole game" once upgrades solved the haggling for them. Co-op raises the ceiling (Schedule I, Supermarket Together, Shift At Midnight) but is not required (TCG Card Shop Simulator reached about 3.5M players solo). By anecdotal estimates it can double the engineering, and it can be impossible to retrofit, so it has to be decided when the concept is chosen. In 2026, supermarket, card shop, food service, drug dealing and internet cafe themes are saturated or faded. The open lanes are twists on inspection, appraisal and flipping, nostalgia retail, bookshops and thrift, delivery, and novelty venues. Choose the concept verb first, ladder second and theme third, and make it pass a paper "ladder test" and a one-week greybox "toy test" before any production art is made. Caveats: most sales figures below are third-party estimates, the review analyses are rough keyword counts over samples of a few hundred reviews, and Steam achievement percentages count every owner, including people who barely played.

## One visible verb and a tight wallet carry the first hours

### Games strip the chore out of chores

Job sims take a real task, remove what makes it a chore, and amplify what makes it satisfying. Psychologist Jamie Madigan summarises it this way: the games "remove the worst of the uncertainty, helplessness, ambiguity, and consequences for failure" of real jobs and give players "clear goals, unambiguous feedback, winnable challenges, and predictable rewards" ([Psychology of Games](https://www.psychologyofgames.com/2017/08/why-do-people-play-jobs/)). Controlled studies point the same way. In a 500-person experiment by Klimmt, Hartmann and Frey, controls that failed a third of the time **significantly cut enjoyment**. The pleasure comes from *effectance*, the feeling that your input changes the world immediately and out of proportion to the effort ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/5762707_Effectance_and_Control_as_Determinants_of_Video_Game_Enjoyment)). A pre-registered CHI 2024 study of **1,699 players** found that curiosity was the strongest predictor of enjoyment and the *only* predictor of how long people chose to keep playing. Feedback tied to real success raised motivation, while amplified feedback on its own *lowered* it ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642656)). Juice works when it tells players whether their action worked. Spectacle that fires regardless is noise.

### PowerWash Simulator is the worked recipe

FuturLab's design director described the target as "an 8/10 for the realism of how you clean but a 4/10 for the realism of what was cleaned". The goal was to recreate the satisfaction of *watching* power-washing videos, not to simulate the job. The rules that followed were concrete. **Contrast comes first**: "nothing is more important than the contrast between the clean and dirty state", so a dark patio table was repainted creamy white. Every object is split into named parts that each finish with a flash and a ding, and the parts follow the edges players expect, because "anticipation and payoff contribute a great deal to the satisfaction experienced". Pipe and power management were cut "to keep the friction of the experience to a minimum" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/level-design-of-powerwash-simulator)). The ding began as a placeholder. Its "distribution" is now designed into every job ([FuturLab](https://www.futurlab.co.uk/news/world-intellectual-property-day-how-did-powerwash-simulator-come-to-be)). Unpacking follows the same subtractive approach: no scores, no fail states and one polished verb ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/unpacking-the-design-pillars-of-a-chill-puzzle-game)).

Steam approval follows how directly the verb produces a visible result. Crime Scene Cleaner sits at **98.1%** ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1040200/)) and PowerWash at **96.4%** ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1290000/)). Cooking Simulator, whose physics-driven hands make the verb itself a source of failure, sits at **84.6%**, and **45% of its 100 most-helpful negative reviews** cite physics or controls ([Steam review sample](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/641320?json=1&language=english&review_type=negative&filter=all&day_range=9223372036854775807&num_per_page=100)). House Flipper 2 lost a 621-hour veteran by making the work harder to see: "can't see what I even did, which was half the fun and satisfaction" ([Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198025287309/recommended/1190970/)).

### Shop sims add scarcity, a clock and a place that changes

Business sims put the verb inside a precarious economy, and players say the scrappy start is the best part. Schedule I's most-helpful negative review says the early loop is fun because you are "constantly on the edge of your seat trying to make ends meet". After that it is "meh", and the reviewer's 92 hours came from having "replayed the beginning like 4 times" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/negativereviews/)). Supermarket Simulator players tell their day one fondly: $200 of stock, only bread and flour on the shelves, prices forgotten, a loan taken ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/positivereviews/)).

A light clock adds pressure without failure; Retro Rewind's business day lasts **6–8 real minutes** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retro_Rewind)). The place itself is the long-term reward: "Watching your station evolve visually across dozens of hours is the single most satisfying thing here" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1149620/positivereviews/)). Schedule I keeps every production step hands-on, because "chores don't feel like chores when you turn them into minigames". Upgraded tools *swap* the minigame rather than delete it ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/schedule-1-is-great-because-it-knows-crafting-sucks-unless-you-turn-everything-into-a-fun-minigame/)).

### The stories players tell are ones they chose

The moments players retell are usually mischief they picked, not events the game imposed. TCG Card Shop's most-helpful positive review (538 votes) is a player entering his own tournament, winning it and pocketing the entry fees ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3070070/positivereviews/)). Dealer's Life 2 players write "10/10 would legally scam again" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1343670/positivereviews/)). Internet Cafe Simulator 2 lets you punch celebrity lookalikes "with basically no consequences" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1563180/positivereviews/)).

Imposed chaos works as spice and fails as upkeep. One critic found ICS2's jank "really kind of entertaining" but its thugs, bombs and power cuts "too much weird stuff happening all the time" ([Half Glass Gaming](https://halfglassgaming.com/2022/01/internet-cafe-simulator-2-so-much-jank-so-much-shame/)). Waterpark Simulator's constant saboteurs drew "stress upon stress" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3293260/negativereviews/)). GameDiscoverCo's summary of the 2025 first-person sim breakouts is a "catchy concept, easy to grok gameplay and supreme streamer-compatibility", with much of the entertainment coming from amusing job failures ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-rise-of-the-first-person-sim)).

## Players leave when money stops buying new decisions

### Few finish, and the games sell anyway

Achievement data shows how few players reach the late game. Only **19.5% of PowerWash owners complete the career** ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1290000/achievements)), 1.5% of House Flipper owners "finish the game" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/613100/achievements/)), and 5.9% of Car Mechanic Simulator 2021 owners reach level 50 ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/1190000/achievements/)). In Supermarket Simulator, 87.9% check out 100 customers by hand, but only **9.7% hire four restockers and 3.0% hire four cashiers** ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/2670630/achievements)). In TCG Card Shop, 41.1% open 1,000 packs and only 11.2% open 10,000 ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/stats/3070070/achievements/)). These games sell because the first 5–20 hours are good, not because people finish them. The commercially critical stretch is shorter still: Steam refunds any game played under two hours within 14 days ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/steam_refunds/)).

### Weak games lose players within hours; the hits hold them for dozens

Samples of up to 500 English Steam reviews per game, pulled for this research, put the median playtime at a negative review at **2–7 hours** for weaker or janky games (Internet Cafe Simulator 1 at 2.0h, Dealer's Life 2 at 6.3h), **8–15 hours** for mid-tier games (Gas Station Simulator, Cash Cleaner, Retro Rewind), and **25–47 hours** for the top hits (Supermarket Simulator, TCG Card Shop, Schedule I, Car Dealer Simulator) (e.g. [Schedule I reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/negativereviews/)).

The hits do not avoid the late-game complaint. They push it 20–30 hours later. Two shapes appear. **Finite, story-led games** (Crime Scene Cleaner, Cooking Simulator, PowerWash 2) lose 25–33% of their negative reviewers in the first two hours. **Open-ended economy games** (Supermarket, TCG, PowerWash 1, House Flipper) lose 25–41% of theirs after 50 hours, from players who liked the game and ran out of novelty ([Steam review sample](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/2670630?json=1&language=english&review_type=negative&filter=all&day_range=9223372036854775807&num_per_page=100)).

### Four triggers end a session for good

**The economy stops pushing.** "When you reached level 20 and have more than 100K money you can remove the game from your computer because nothing more to do" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1190000/discussions/0/5503948370881709179)). Schedule I players say "it feels like the target is to make more and more money for no reason" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/negativereviews/)), and its top Kingpin ranks unlock nothing ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/schedule-1-ranks-unlocks/)).

**The last 1% becomes a hunt.** PowerWash players complain about "fences you have to hit at 360 different angles" ([Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198035503422/recommended/1290000/)). Crime Scene Cleaner's final levels become "hide and seek with small items scattered around massive areas" ([Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561198064336743/recommended/1040200/)).

**A mastered chore cannot be delegated.** Supermarket players expected that "when you build it big... owner work less", but restockers can't keep up and ordering can never be automated ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/discussions/1/7607215340156983139/?l=english)). TCG players beg: "Let me hire someone to rip open packs" ([Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197999029630/recommended/3070070/)).

**Staff turn the owner into a babysitter.** This is the genre's worst-reviewed moment. Motel Manager "utterly falls apart" once you depend on staff, about 8–10 hours in ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2594540/negativereviews/)). Grocery Store Simulator's most-voted complaint (124 votes) is that "employees can't even use the forklifts" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2961880/negativereviews/)). Waterpark players say "You will never be able to hire enough people" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3293260/negativereviews/)).

Automation that goes too far fails just as badly. Dealer's Life 1 players hire every expert after about three hours and then "skip 90% of your customers without even bargaining" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/982270/negativereviews/)). Cafe Owner Simulator reduces the player to "the cashier pressing the pictures of what the customer wants" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1498140/negativereviews/)).

### Neglect and greed cost even the hits

Abandonment drives most Mixed reviews: **60%** of Fast Food Simulator's sampled negatives mention it ("gets boring after 20–30 hour mark because no more updates") ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2916430/negativereviews/)), and Mall Simulator's "gameplay loop lasts about 15 minutes before you realize there's nothing else to do" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3180150/negativereviews/)). Paid DLC is the top complaint category in the most-helpful negatives of PowerWash (52%), House Flipper (69%) and Supermarket Simulator (48%). Supermarket's seven paid DLCs briefly dragged its recent reviews down to 68% ([Game Marketing](https://gamemarketing.substack.com/p/how-retro-rewind-sold-200000-copies)).

The director's reference fits this pattern. **Internet Cafe Simulator 2 sits at 73.4% all-time but 60% over the last 30 days**, ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1563180/)), and 39% of sampled negative reviews mention bugs. Players add that the "devs ditched the game" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1563180/negativereviews/)). Retention can be repaired, though. One Supermarket quality-of-life patch brought back a lapsed 291-hour player who went "from finding the store management frustrating and tedious to actually enjoying" it again ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/positivereviews/)).

## The next level pulls only when it changes the work

### Rungs that add verbs keep players; rungs that add zeros lose them

The director believes the fun in Scritchy Scratchy and Dealer's Life comes from wanting to reach the next level and get better. The evidence supports that, with a condition the theory explains. Raph Koster argues that fun is learning, and that a pattern the brain has fully "chunked" becomes boring ([Lost Garden](https://lostgarden.com/2005/05/08/book-raph-kosters-theory-of-fun-for-game-design/)). Salen and Zimmerman call an action meaningful only when its result is "discernable" and "integrated" into later play ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meaningful_play)). Kongregate's Anthony Pecorella, who designs idle games for a living, says "the novelty of big numbers on their own is largely gone" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-math-of-idle-games-part-iii)).

The games bear this out:

| Game | What a step up adds | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scritchy Scratchy | New ticket catalogues "each with its own quirks, surprises, and sometimes downsides"; prestige perks "that reshape strategy" | 94% positive, about $2M Steam gross (estimate) | [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3948120/Scritchy_Scratchy/); [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/3948120/Scritchy_Scratchy/sales-revenue) |
| Balatro | Boss blinds that change the rules ("Must play 5 cards", "-1 Hand Size") on top of published score targets | Rule-changing layers were added after a plain scoring core | [Balatro Wiki](https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes); [LocalThunk](https://localthunk.com/blog/balatro-timeline-3aarh) |
| Schedule I | 6 regions with new customers; new drugs with new production minigames; employees | Strong mid-game; the top ranks unlock nothing and "make more and more money for no reason" | [Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/schedule-1-ranks-unlocks/); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/negativereviews/) |
| Gas Station Simulator | Each popularity level opens a service with its own minigame (tyres, car wash, workshop) | $10M+ Steam revenue on a ~$111K budget | [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1149620/); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/04/22/gas-station-simulator-revenue-10-million-steam) |
| Supermarket Simulator | Mostly more products on more shelves | Late game becomes a "stockroom simulator" | [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2670630/discussions/1/7607215340156983139/?l=english) |
| Storage Hunter Simulator | Faster vehicle, nicer homes, upgrades costing about 4× more | "the mechanics dont evolve in any way"; 78% positive | [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1442430/negativereviews/) |
| Pharmacy Simulator | First new medicines after about 100 customers and an exam | "repetitive within 20 minutes"; Mixed | [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2930320/negativereviews/) |

In roguelite terms, players "need to feel that every run moves them forward in a way that changes how they play, not just how strong they are" ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-design-a-roguelite-meta-progression)). The working rule is that every rung must add at least one new verb, place, customer type, threat or risk profile. "Same thing, ×10" never counts as a rung.

### "Getting better" has to mean the player gets better

Dealer's Life shows both halves of the director's idea in one game. At its best it is a skill game: "The goods' real value is never shown anywhere", customers' traits show in their appearance, and there are "No in-game guides" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/982270/)). Then the shop's power replaced the player's skill. With competence at 8 or higher, fakes stop mattering ([GameGrin](https://www.gamegrin.com/reviews/dealers-life-2-review/)). The most-voted negative review (139 votes) says "You don't need skill or employee. Buy it anyway... you will find another idiot who will pay 100k more" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1343670/negativereviews/)). Another says "You watch numbers go up. That's the whole game" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1343670/negativereviews/)). In the sample, **25% of Dealer's Life 2 negatives say "boring"**, the highest rate of any game sampled, and critics put the cliff at "5–6 hours" ([Softpedia](https://www.softpedia.com/reviews/games/pc/dealer-039-s-life-2-review-534955.shtml)).

The same failure appears wherever a formula or an upgrade solves the judgement call. Supermarket Together players publish a pricing formula ("market price × 2 − 0.01") ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2709570/positivereviews/)). Retro Rewind removed pricing entirely and was criticised for it (62 votes) ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3552140/negativereviews/)). House Flipper 2 hands out the best scraper at the start "with no upgrade options" ([Steam review](https://steamcommunity.com/profiles/76561197992297217/recommended/1190970/)). Schedule I, by contrast, keeps pricing a per-customer worry about "undervaluing my own product" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/schedule-1-is-great-because-it-knows-crafting-sucks-unless-you-turn-everything-into-a-fun-minigame/)). Balatro publishes its targets, so a better player gets further with the same cards ([Balatro Wiki](https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes)). The design rule is that upgrades sell *better information at a cost* and never the answer.

### Loss must be real, bounded and chosen

Progress feels earned when something is at stake. In Balatro, missing a blind ends the run ([Balatro Wiki](https://balatrowiki.org/w/Blinds_and_Antes)). In Scritchy Scratchy, some tickets "threaten entire runs with bankruptcy" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3948120/Scritchy_Scratchy/)). Schedule I players ask for *more* danger, and "police raids" was one of three options in its first community update vote ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/schedule-1-final-release-is-three-years-away-according-to-its-developer-first-major-update-will-be-community-voted/)). The opposite complaint is just as clear. Car Mechanic Simulator's expert mode lets players "rocket to level 50 and lots of cash with no real risk of any failure" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1190000/discussions/0/3037104113120401641)).

The tension with cozy design resolves the same way in every successful example. Project Horseshoe's cozy-games report lists danger and *mandatory* responsibility as cozy-breakers ([Lost Garden](https://lostgarden.com/2018/01/24/cozy-games/)). So risk works when it is opt-in and survivable. Balatro risks the run, not the save, and Scritchy's bankruptcy feeds a prestige reset rather than wiping the player out ([Adventure Gamers](https://adventuregamers.com/article/scritchy-scratchy-game)). Too much imposed risk, as in Waterpark Simulator, reads as stress rather than stakes.

### A named goal, a promotion, and a fresh scrappy start

The best ladders show the next goal by name. Balatro shows the next score and the boss's rule before you play it. Scritchy puts the "Final Chance" ticket and its price on the menu from the start ([Adventure Gamers](https://adventuregamers.com/article/scritchy-scratchy-game)).

Automation works when it reads as a promotion: "You go from a desperate gambler to a casino manager" ([NeonLightsMedia](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/scritchy-scratchy-review)). TCG Card Shop's solo developer designed the order on purpose. Players are hands-on early, then get "tools to make the management part easier or more automated, so they get more time for the collecting part" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/solo-developer-on-creating-a-simulation-game-about-a-local-game-store)).

Prestige keeps old content fresh when the replay is both faster and different. A Scritchy run "that took you five hours on your first attempt might take thirty minutes on your second", and credits arrive at 7–10 hours ([NeonLightsMedia](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/scritchy-scratchy-review)). Put this next to the Schedule I player who replayed the opening four times, and a design target follows: **each macro step should bring back the tight-wallet tension of the first hour, under new rules**, through a new branch, district or venue rather than a bigger version of the same shop.

Two caveats apply. Scritchy is a short $6.99 incremental and Dealer's Life is a haggling game, not a walk-around first-person sim. Their ladder lessons transfer, but their length and price do not.

## Co-op multiplies a working loop and rescues nothing

### Co-op raises the ceiling but is optional

The market evidence for co-op is strong. Among Steam games grossing over $100K, the share mentioning co-op rose from **3.2% in 2022 to 5.8% in 2023**, and GameDiscoverCo concluded that "making co-op games is hard(er), but those who can reap the dividends" ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-game-descriptions-tell-us-about)). Multiplayer games took the top six slots in Steam's 2025 sales, with Schedule I at #4 ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/steam-top-selling-2025-friendslop-rpgs-sales-2000654157)), and Schedule I peaked at **459,075 concurrent players** ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/3164500/charts/)). Supermarket Together, free and built around the rule "The more players in your supermarket, the more demanding customers will become", now posts monthly peaks of about **10–12K, roughly double those of the paid Supermarket Simulator** ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/2709570); [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/2670630)). Shift At Midnight, a $10 game with co-op for up to three players, sold close to **1M copies in July 2026** ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/july-2026s-top-new-pc-and-console)). Storage Hunter's publisher called multiplayer the "most requested feature" and added 4-player co-op at 1.0 ([astragon](https://www.astragon.com/news/detail/storage-hunter-simulator-hits-10-tomorrow)).

Solo hits are just as real, though. TCG Card Shop Simulator went to 1.0 single-player with about **3.5M players** ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3070070/TCG_Card_Shop_Simulator/); [Danamic](https://danamic.org/2026/09/21/one-malaysian-dev-3-5-million-copies-tcg-card-shop-simulator-finally-lets-you-duel/)), Crime Scene Cleaner passed **1M copies** ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1040200/view/523103429639275286)), and Gas Station Simulator passed **$10M** in Steam revenue without co-op ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/04/22/gas-station-simulator-revenue-10-million-steam)).

### Co-op added after launch brings players back only briefly

| Game | Before | Co-op month | Afterwards | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supermarket Simulator (4-player update, Oct 2025) | 5,573 peak (Sep 2025) | 10,185 peak | 5,790 peak by Dec 2025 | [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/2670630) |
| House Flipper 2 (free co-op DLC plus free weekend, May 2025) | 1,515 avg / 2,852 peak | 3,808 avg / 21,829 peak | ~2,400–3,000 avg | [SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/1190970) |

Co-op added late worked as a one-month event, not a relaunch. For House Flipper 2 the free weekend muddies the effect. Retrofitting can also be impossible. Gas Station Simulator's team says "the game wasn't designed as a co-op game, so adding that feature isn't possible at this stage" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1149620/discussions/0/599660572366119529/)). Car Dealer Simulator's developers said co-op would mean rebuilding the game ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2404880/discussions/0/715610102638318415/)).

### Why a job is more fun with friends

The friend-group hits are almost all jobs: scrap quotas, deliveries, dealing. Their fun is not the job but the shared, failure-prone way it gets done. Players say "playing with a friend makes everything funnier... watching the whole place fall apart" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2916430/positivereviews/)). PC Gamer praised Schedule I co-op for letting players "bully my friends into doing the hard parts" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/sim/schedule-1-is-my-favourite-co-op-game-of-the-year-because-it-lets-me-bully-my-friends-into-doing-the-hard-parts-for-me/)).

The ingredients that carry over to a business sim are role splitting, demand that scales with player count, a shared quota or stake, and a reveal worth watching. Proximity voice also carries over, because a first-person shop is a 3D space you walk around. There are two flavours. **Chaos-together** (Supermarket Together, Schedule I) has the higher ceiling. **Cozy parallel play**, which FuturLab described as "a relaxing non-competitive environment" for PowerWash, is calmer ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/powerwash-simulator-co-op/)). The "friendslop" model of a $5–10 game that is "unenjoyable when played alone" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendslop)) is a different business. Its solo players call it "way too hard and punitive" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1966720/discussions/0/4032473436328346677/)).

### What co-op costs a small Unity team

Licensing is cheap. Schedule I ships on the free FishNet library ([s1modding](https://s1modding.github.io/docs/moddevs/resources/)) and Lethal Company on Unity's Netcode for GameObjects ([lethal.wiki](https://lethal.wiki/dev/advanced/networking)). Steam's relay network carries peer-to-peer traffic with no stated fee ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/multiplayer/steamdatagramrelay)). Photon, by contrast, bills by concurrent users ([Photon](https://www.photonengine.com/pun/pricing)).

Engineering is the expensive part. One widely cited forum estimate says multiplayer "is likely to take at least twice as much effort as the rest of the work on your game combined", and that "it MUST be planned from the beginning" ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/how-to-decide-if-your-game-should-could-be-multiplayer/754372)). That is anecdote, not measurement. The documented failures are more specific. **Host-only progress** is the top source of anger: Schedule I guests lose unlocks and see desynced employees ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3164500/discussions/1/599650563430319444/)). **Desync grows with the shop**: Supermarket Together needs a host with a "decent stable connection" above 70 NPCs ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2709570/discussions/0/597388550651017225/)). **Physics sync** was the hardest part of R.E.P.O. ([Photon](https://blog.photonengine.com/r-e-p-o-multiplayer-success-powered-by-photon/)). PowerWash 2 answered the most common complaint by saving career progress for both players ([co-op.gg](https://www.co-op.gg/game/powerwash-simulator-2)). One project-specific cost also applies. Unity runs only on Urfan's laptop, not in the cloud sessions, so every multiplayer test means two clients on his hardware. The QA burden falls on the director's evenings.

The position this evidence supports: **decide co-op when choosing the concept, not later.** Prefer a concept that works solo and gets better with a second player through role splitting. If the concept passes that test, build the Unity architecture network-ready from the first line, prove the fun solo, and ship 2–4 player co-op at or before 1.0 rather than as a retrofit 17–20 months after launch. Keep synced physics out of the core verb.

## Supermarkets are taken; inspection, appraisal and nostalgia are open

### The 2026 theme map

Shop-sim themes are winner-take-all. One or two early entrants take almost all the attention, and later reskins earn a small fraction. The table summarises a Steam search sweep of 2024–26 releases with the theme word in the name. Review counts are English-only, which undercounts global titles by about 1.5–2×, and release counts are lower bounds.

| Status | Themes | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Saturated, winner-take-all** | Supermarket and grocery; card and collectibles shop; drugs and crime | Supermarket: 47+ releases, both leaders from 2024, later entries under a tenth of their reviews. Card shop: TCG Card Shop at 33,978 English reviews against 1,641 for the runner-up. Crime: Schedule I has about 40× the reviews of the next title ([Steam search](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=supermarket); [Steam search](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=card%20shop)) |
| **Crowded, low hit rate** | Cafe, restaurant and fast food; bakery | 193+ cafe/restaurant/fast-food releases, none at 5,000 English reviews ([Steam search](https://store.steampowered.com/search/?term=restaurant)) |

The internet cafe theme itself is a dead end. Internet Cafe Simulator 2 is better used as a **structural model**: a small venue under rent and debt pressure, a tech tree that forks toward "business prodigy or a brawler", a legit-or-illegal path, and chaos events ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1563180/)). Its bugs, its abandonment and the constant chaos are the parts to leave behind.

### A theme only works when it brings its own verb

Theme alone does not set a game apart. PC Gamer counted **14 retail sims released in one week** that "all kinda look the same" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/steam-week-in-review-a-torrent-of-janky-retail-sims-continues-to-flood-steam-and-theres-no-end-in-sight/)). In August 2025, only one of 38 "Simulator" launches cleared $70K gross ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-rise-of-the-first-person-sim)).

The 2024–26 winners paired a theme with a verb only that theme has. **Retro Rewind**, from a two-person team, rewinds tapes and sold 100K copies in four days after a 137-day demo ([Game Marketing](https://gamemarketing.substack.com/p/how-retro-rewind-sold-200000-copies)). **Cash Cleaner** launders bills "literally"; its team chose it over a laundromat sim because "IF the joke landed, it had viral potential", and its median playtime reached three times their prediction ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-cash-cleaner-simulator-hit-100k)). **TCG Card Shop** rips packs, and it rode a real trading-card boom ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/tcg-card-shop-simulator-the-second)). PowerWash's designers credit much of their success to the verb being "so clearly implied by the game's name" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/level-design-of-powerwash-simulator)). Chris Zukowski, who studies Steam marketing, notes that Steam players especially like simulating blue-collar jobs ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/04/11/the-hit-games-of-early-2025/)). He also warns that "Too many Indies turn their nose up at Job simulators… Players really like these games" ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/12/29/2026-games-of-the-year/)). Simulation ranked **#2 among Steam genres in 2025**, with 4.1% of releases reaching 1,000 reviews ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2026/01/27/what-the-hell-happened-in-2025/)).

### Cheap-to-build themes attract the clones

Asset cost explains much of the crowding. A complete supermarket-sim code template sells for **$90** on the Unity Asset Store ([Unity Asset Store](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/templates/systems/store-simulator-supermarket-game-template-309463)), and supermarkets, cafes, gas stations and motels are all well covered by store packs. Themes whose product is a large catalogue of distinct items (antiques, cards, VHS covers, auction loot) cost more to build, which is part of why they remain open.

Presentation matters as well. Recognisable store assets invite "asset flip" accusations ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/dont-buy-recognizable-asset-store-assets-it-will-be-seen-as-an-asset-flip/928444)). One study of about 10,000 games released in 2025 found that those disclosing generative AI got **about 52.6% fewer reviews**, though the absolute numbers were tiny: an average of 4 against 7 in the first month ([Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/steam-games-that-disclose-ai-use-may-sell-far-less-new-study-finds/)). A team built on AI developers needs a hand-directed look more than most.

### What success looks like in numbers

The lane still pays better than Steam as a whole. Across 107 3D shop sims released since 2020, median net revenue was **$24,185 against $749 for all of Steam**, with $19.99+ games clustered among the top earners ([Game Marketing](https://gamemarketing.substack.com/p/how-retro-rewind-sold-200000-copies)). That sample is likely filtered toward games with some traction.

The earlier container research recommended staying under $10 because wishlists convert at about **0.10× above $10 against 0.17×** for games with 10K+ wishlists ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)). For a 15–25 hour first-person sim the arithmetic flips. At $19.99, 0.10 converted copies per wishlist earns more than 0.17 per wishlist at $7.99 (about $2.00 against $1.36 of list price per wishlist, by our calculation). The lane's $14.99–19.99 norm is therefore the right target.

Realistic planning bands for a small team (our estimates, using lifetime units of about 30× English reviews): the median release sells low thousands of copies; a good result is 1,000–5,000 reviews, or about 30K–150K units; a breakout is 200K–1M+. Every breakout had a one-sentence hook and a demo or prologue that built momentum. Parcel Simulator's demo took it from 7,000 to 42,000 wishlists ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/08/26/the-demo-effect-from-7000-wishlists-to-42000/)). Pre-fest wishlists remain the strongest predictor of Next Fest gains (r = 0.825) ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/03/26/benchmarks-how-many-wishlists-can-i-get-from-steam-next-fest/)).

## Conclusion

The research changes the director's question in one useful way. "Wanting to reach the next level" is the right engine, but the level players are chasing is really **the next thing to learn**, and the most fun hour in nearly every one of these games is the first: tight money, a new verb and nothing yet solved. Players replay Schedule I's opening, Scritchy's prestige runs are loved for being faster *and* different, and Dealer's Life dies at the moment it stops asking for judgement. The design problem, then, is not to make numbers grow. It is to recreate the first hour's conditions at every tier: a fresh scarcity, a new rule and a skill the shop's upgrades cannot replace. The hits do not escape the late-game wall; they move it 20–30 hours further out. A small team should therefore plan a finite, well-paced 15–25 hour arc that ends before the wall, then extend it with free updates, rather than promise an endless sandbox it cannot fill.

The second shift concerns how a concept should be chosen. The 2026 breakouts are not new loops. They are the proven first-person shop loop with **one verb only that theme has**, **one twist with emotional charge** (nostalgia, a joke, a moral edge, dread), and often a second player. That makes concept selection a filtering problem that can be done cheaply. A concept that cannot name its verb, cannot write eight rule-changing tiers on paper, or bores testers as a greybox toy will not be rescued by theme, art or co-op. The container prototype is a reminder of the order of operations: an economy that simulates cleanly is not evidence of fun. The toy has to be tested first.

## Principles for choosing and testing our concept

1. **Choose the verb before the theme.** Each candidate names one physical first-person action that changes something visible within about a second, finishes in small named pieces with their own sound, and belongs to that theme (rip a pack, rewind a tape, launder a bill, appraise a fake). Reject anything whose core verb is "stock a shelf" or "click a menu".
2. **Pass the one-sentence and two-second tests.** The pitch must fit one sentence that implies the verb, as "PowerWash Simulator" does. A silent two-second clip must show both the stake and the result. Prefer hooks with a joke or a moral edge (Cash Cleaner, Schedule I, Probably Stolen) and a familiar, blue-collar job.
3. **Stay out of saturated lanes unless the twist is the product.** Skip supermarket, card shop, food service, bakery, drug-crime, straight internet cafe and the franchise-held lanes. Shortlist from inspection and "spot the bad customer", appraisal and flipping, nostalgia retail, bookshops and thrift, delivery and mail, and novelty venues, or a cross between two of them. Use Internet Cafe Simulator 2 as a structural model (small venue, rent and debt, a legit-or-illegal fork, occasional chaos), not as the theme.
4. **Write the ladder on paper before building anything.** List the first eight tiers. Each must add at least one new verb, place, customer type, threat or risk profile. Reject any concept that cannot produce eight rule-changing tiers on paper.
5. **Name the player's skill and protect it.** State what the player gets better at (reading customers, pricing, spotting fakes, routing, layout) and show that no upgrade removes the judgement. Upgrades sell better information at a cost, never answers. Prices stay in the player's hands.
6. **Keep money tight and loss real but survivable.** Have rent, debt or a quota due each in-game week. Bad buys, theft and raids cost real money. A bust converts into progress, such as a restart with permanent perks, and never wipes the save. Risky options are opt-in.
7. **Bring back the scrappy start at each macro step.** A new branch, district or venue should restore "barely making rent" under new rules. Prestige, if used, must be faster *and* different, with its gain previewed before the player commits.
8. **Plan automation before building it.** For each verb, decide whether it stays manual (the signature verb), becomes delegable once it is boring (logistics, restocking, ordering), or gets a tool that changes it. Staff must work reliably and give the player more of the fun verb, not more babysitting. Each automation should feel like a promotion with a new hands-on job attached.
9. **Leave room for player-chosen mischief, and keep imposed chaos rare and answerable.** Let players overcharge, bluff, rig and chase off shoplifters. Every imposed event comes with a funny tool to answer it. If the concept uses randomness, the project's honest-odds rule still applies.
10. **Decide co-op when choosing the concept.** Ask whether a second player creates role splitting, demand that scales with player count, and a shared stake. If yes, build the Unity project network-ready from the first day: one authority for state and randomness, every action as a command, serialisable state, and progress kept by each player. Then target 2–4 player co-op at or before 1.0. If no, ship solo with the same clean architecture. In both cases, keep synced physics out of the core verb.
11. **Size and price for the lane.** Aim for a 15–25 hour arc at $14.99–19.99. Put the first rule-changing unlock inside about 90 minutes, well within the two-hour refund window. Publish a roadmap of free updates, and never sell paid DLC that looks like split-off updates.
12. **Run the fun test in three cheap stages, with two or three concepts in parallel.**
    - **(a) Paper, half a day per concept:** the one-sentence pitch, a description of the two-second clip, the eight-tier ladder, the skill statement, and a rough spreadsheet economy if one is needed.
    - **(b) Toy, up to five days per concept:** a Unity greybox built from primitives and placeholder sounds that contains only the verb, with honest juice (high contrast, a completion sound per piece) and no goals or money. Show five players the controls, give them ten minutes, then say "stop whenever you like". Record how many minutes they keep going by choice, and note what they say without being asked.
    - **(c) Loop, about one week for the surviving concept:** add one 6–8 minute day with a tight wallet, one judgement call and one tier swap that changes a rule. Run 5–10 players through a 15-minute required session, then free play. Ask "what did you want to unlock next?" and "what would you do differently tomorrow?" Run one session with two people sharing a screen, one playing and one back-seat, then swapping, to gauge co-op and streamer value.
13. **Set kill thresholds before testing, and keep to them.** Our proposal (no studio publishes such thresholds):
    - At the toy stage, most testers keep playing at least ten extra minutes without being asked.
    - At the loop stage, at least half name a specific next goal or a skill they would improve.
    - Testers notice the tier swap without being told.
    - Urfan wants to keep playing.
    
    Kill anything that misses and move on. Of Supercell's ten most recent games at the time of one report, seven were killed in prototype ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/quality-is-worth-killing-for-supercell-s-ruthless-approach-to-production)), and the seven-day prototyping rules are explicit: "Build the Toy First", "Make It Juicy", "cut your losses and move on" ([Gabler et al.](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~markk/SWE_copies/gabler_prototyping.html)).
14. **Test the market as early as the fun.** Once a concept survives, show a short silent clip of the greybox verb to strangers and open the Steam page early. Plan a demo at least a month before a Next Fest that lets players reach the first tier change or automation (top demos hold players for 80 minutes to 3 hours).
15. **Make it look made by people.** Give the game a distinctive name and a hand-directed art direction, even when it is built from store packs. Use no AI art on the store page, and show visible update cadence after launch.

## Risks

1. **Saturation and fast clones.** Retail sims arrive at more than a dozen a week, and viral hooks get cloned within days. Only depth, a distinctive verb and an early store page protect a concept.
2. **The late-game wall arrives anyway.** Even the biggest hits draw "nothing to do" reviews after 25–47 hours, and a small team cannot out-produce PlayWay on content. A finite arc plus kept promises on updates is the realistic defence.
3. **NPC, staff and physics bugs are the top review killers,** and first-person customer AI is hard to get right. Because Unity runs only on Urfan's laptop, testing of NPC behaviour, and later multiplayer, falls on his evenings.
4. **Co-op scope can blow up.** Anecdotal estimates put multiplayer at 1.5–3× the engineering, with doubled QA and desync that worsens as the shop grows.
5. **The "AI slop" label.** A team built on AI developers in a genre known for AI capsule art must show visible craft. Disclosed-AI games drew roughly half the reviews of comparable games.
6. **Hot twists can cool by launch.** Inspection-horror and appraisal are the hottest lanes of 2026. Probably Stolen launches on 28 Oct 2026, and several auction games are in the pipeline, so a 2027 launch may face crowding there too.
7. **The data is uncertain.** Sales figures are mostly third-party estimates that sometimes conflict (Storage Hunter's estimates range from about 84K to 547K copies, against a developer-reported 250K). Review themes come from keyword counts, and achievement percentages count every owner, including people who barely played.
